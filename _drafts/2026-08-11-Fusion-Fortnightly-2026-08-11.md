- [ITER installed its sixth sector module nearly six months ahead of schedule, with Korea’s final vacuum-vessel sector helping pull the projected final-module installation forward from December to mid-2027.]{https://www.iter.org/two-thirds-iter-tokamak-core-now-place}. ITER reported July 29 that the roughly 1,100-ton module, built around vacuum-vessel sector No. 1, was lowered into the tokamak pit on July 28; that sector was the fourth and final one manufactured by South Korea, while Europe is responsible for the other five. Each module integrates a roughly 440-ton vacuum-vessel sector, thermal shielding, and two roughly 310-ton toroidal-field coils to millimeter tolerances, making this a major heavy-manufacturing and integration supply-chain milestone. ITER says accumulated assembly experience has improved predictability enough to advance the anticipated final-sector installation from December 2027 to mid-2027, reducing schedule risk on a critical path, though it did not give a corresponding cost saving.

- [Germany committed about €125 million to three fusion hubs designed explicitly to build industrial supply chains for stellarator components, laser systems and targets, neutron-resistant materials, breeding blankets, and tritium handling.]{https://www.bmftr.bund.de/SharedDocs/Kurzmeldungen/DE/2026/07/290726-fusionshubs.html}. Announced by Germany’s Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space on July 29, with funding beginning in August, STRIDE will develop and test magnetic-fusion components and industrial supply chains; VEGA will work on lasers, targets, diagnostics, shared test infrastructure, and an eventual mass-production value chain; and MAT-TRIx will address neutron-resistant materials, tritium-breeding blankets, tritium handling, and realistic component qualification. - [TAE Technologies secured a prospective helium-3 supply option from Black Moon Energy, exposing both the strategic value and extreme immaturity of an alternative fusion-fuel supply chain.]{https://tae.com/tae-technologies-and-black-moon-energy-corporation-enter-into-strategic-helium-3-supply-and-commercialization-agreement-for-fusion-power/}. - [ORNL licensed Type One Energy four cryogenic pellet technologies to address a concrete D-T fuel-cycle bottleneck: no existing injector can yet make pellets at the rates required for reactor operation.]{https://typeoneenergy.com/insights-announcements/ornl-type-one-energy-to-advance-fusion-fuel-cycle-with-innovative-new-technologies/}. The agreement was signed August 5 and published August 6, covering a tritium-compatible pellet gas gun, cryogenic screw extruder, sub-20-kelvin pressure-relief device, and closed-loop extrusion-recirculation system. ORNL says tritium’s scarcity and radioactivity make containment and reprocessing unusually difficult and that current pellet injectors cannot meet reactor-rate requirements; the licensed design aims to increase production rate while recycling excess fuel, reducing tritium inventory, and eliminating some external gas-handling equipment. The immediate scope is Type One Energy’s U.S. stellarator program, but the underlying constraint applies broadly to continuously fueled D-T plants; license economics, deployment cost, and an operational date were not disclosed.

## Analytical read-through

The **CFS appointment is the most commercially significant** of the three even though it was disclosed after Kim had already started. CFS paired the public introduction of a former Moderna CFO with a $1 billion institutional-heavy financing round and a stated push into ARC development; Kim’s mix of public-market, investment-banking, venture, and operating-company experience fits a company whose next financing problems are likely to look increasingly like large-scale industrial and infrastructure finance.
