## Companies, financing, and commercial development

[ENN reports proton-boron fusion reactions on Xuanlong-50U](https://www.eeo.com.cn/2026/0928/1050796.shtml) and [begins construction on Helong-2](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/china-begins-building-hydrogen-boron-fusion-device); both machines are spherical tokamaks. ENN says Xuanlong-50U produced more than 10⁸ proton-boron fusion reactions/s (about 0.14 mW of fusion power, a 1 GW fusion source would require roughly 7×10²⁰ p-B reactions/s), claiming to be the first to demonstrate p-B fusion on a commercial fusion company’s own machine. For comparison, the recent p-B results on LHD were about 10¹⁴ reactions/s. Helong-2 broke ground September 15 and is intended to pursue electricity generation by 2030, on a similar time-scale to what TAE proposes. As with TAE, the large gap in present performance and what's needed for a power plant gives the date low credibility.

[First Light Fusion reports compression results for FLARE](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/flare-validation-multi-shell-fuel-compression-jonathan-skidmore-suxbe), indicating they achieved the compression expected from simulations.

[Type One Energy completes the first phase of DOE’s Milestone-Based Fusion Development Program](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/type-one-energy-has-completed-the-first-phase-ugcPost-7508564481640165376-hp4i/). Type One says it completed all seven science, technology, and commercial milestones in its first budget period.

[EnableFusion closes a ₩45 billion Series A](https://www.hellodd.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=113270). Quantum Ventures Korea led the round, which brings total funding to approximately ₩56 billion ($42 million). The company is working on precision manufacturing, AI, high-temperature superconductors, and tritium fuel systems for fusion.

[StandardX raises £10 million to produce medical isotopes and tritium](https://www.standardx.tech/news-stories/standardx-launches-with-ps10m-seed-round-to-create-the-rare-isotopes-needed-to-advance-medical-and-fusion-innovation). Initial medical-isotope deliveries are planned for 2027.

[Daedal Systems raises $4.04 million for plasma diagnostics](https://www.daedalsystems.com/funding-announcement). The round will fund the company’s first three diagnostic systems and deployments with pilot customers. 

[Marvel Fusion spins out Caelora to commercialize lasers](https://www.marvelfusion.com/newsroom/marvel-fusion-launches-caelora). The subsidiary will develop diode-pumped lasers for fusion and scientific research, plus radiation sources for medical, industrial, and security applications. 

[nT-Tao and Orion Nuclear agree to explore U.S. fusion deployments](https://www.utilitydive.com/press-release/20260921-nt-tao-and-orion-nuclear-energy-corporation-announce-strategic-collaboratio/). Orion is a newish small entity set up to do primarily small modular fission reactors. 

## Supply chain, manufacturing, and enabling technology

[SPARC’s cryogenic system reaches operational temperature of 8 Kelvin](https://blog.cfs.energy/cryogenics-how-well-make-our-fusion-machine-magnets-colder-than-pluto/). This was a fun plant to design. To make SPARC as small as we could, we removed as much nuclear shielding of the superconducting magnets as we could. This means that a lot of the 140 MW of fusion power goes into the superconducting magnets during the 10 s pulses, which is uncharacteristic of a fusion power plant where the magnets are well shielded (driven by radiation damage requirements) and the fusion power getting to them is much lower. Two MIT [masters](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/4289bb1c-99cb-44bc-87e0-29a86798bdad) [theses](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/a2ebaf13-869b-4551-840e-7df2fe18df12) we written on early concepts of the SPARC cryogenic system.

[Inertia, the company founded by the owner of The Onion, celebrates key milestone: people who make lasers have agreed to discuss making more lasers](https://inertia.com/press/inertia-announces-collaborations-with-semiconductor-industry-leaders). A key part of Inertia's value proposition is decreasing the cost of diode lasers and associated equipment as well as scaling up the manufacturing volume by large factors.

[Renaissance Fusion, DIFFER, and VDL to develop fusion manufacturing partnership](https://www.differ.nl/news/renaissancefusion-vdl-differ). 

[Fusion for Energy publishes six reports mapping fusion technology and supply-chain gaps](https://fusionforenergy.europa.eu/news/fusion-technology-mapping-reports/). Topics covered: fuel cycle, magnets, additive manufacturing, metrology and reverse engineering, artificial intelligence and plasma-facing components. Haven't had the time to go through them myself, but F4E typically produces quality work so it's worth a look if this is what you are interested in.

## Legal and intellectual property

[New retrospective details how IP disputes slowed DOE’s fusion milestone program](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28398v1). Three former DOE officials describe months of stalled negotiations over government patent and data rights. DOE ultimately narrowed those rights and replaced march-in rights with conditional licenses, but companies still reduced or dropped technical milestones to avoid IP entanglements. Not quite the intended effect of a program meant to accelerate commercialization.

[American Fusion says it now has 151 pending U.S. patent applications](https://americanfusionenergy.com/news/american-fusion-inc-otcqb-amfn-provides-corporate-update-on-intellectual-property-texatron-testing-and-national-exchange-uplisting/). Up from the 130 reported last fortnight: the company announced another 21 applications on September 22, covering variations in Texatron’s materials, geometry, and components. These remain applications, not granted patents.

[Tibbar Plasma Technologies’ advanced-fuel fusion-reactor patent application is published](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20260290629). The application describes oscillating plasma rings that repeatedly collide, including hydrogen-boron configurations, with direct conversion of charged fusion products into electricity. The filing acknowledges NSF funding and government rights in the invention. This is a newly published application, not a patent grant.

## Papers and research

[TAE](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1741-4326/ae96c0).

[LLNL and Inertia publish target designs projecting fusion gains of 26–43](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19752). The preprint describes indirect-drive targets predicted to produce 265–427 MJ of fusion energy from a 10 MJ laser pulse. The designs scale up NIF’s approach while addressing manufacturing imperfections and power-plant requirements. These are simulated target gains, not demonstrated performance or net electricity.

[Tritium-lean startup could substantially reduce the breeding requirements for early fusion plants](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352179126001262). Niek Lopes Cardozo and Erin van Rheenen find that relying on each generation of fusion plants to breed startup tritium for the next would require impractically high overbreeding. They instead propose intermittent, tritium-lean startup that could bring a plant to full operation within months with much less initial tritium. If the model holds up, maximizing blanket TBR may be less important than commonly assumed.

[AI brings turbulent transport directly into stellarator design optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28730). A surrogate trained on more than 200,000 gyrokinetic simulations spanning about 23,000 stellarator configurations makes millisecond transport predictions. That lets researchers optimize turbulent heat losses across the plasma rather than rely on indirect proxies. The reported improvements remain computational, with a simplified electron model.

[Reactor-scale stellarator study finds neutron wall loading sets the size floor](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1741-4326/aea918). A *Nuclear Fusion* systems study couples a SQuID stellarator design to the PROCESS plant model and finds that neutron wall loading, rather than magnetic field, becomes the main constraint on shrinking an HTS machine. Even with an optimistic 1.5 MW/m² wall-load limit, the modeled 1 GWe plant has a 16.5 m major radius. HTS beats low-temperature superconductors economically only if its material costs remain below roughly five times LTS costs, assuming comparable manufacturing complexity and risk. Better magnets do not make the rest of the plant disappear.

[Larger fuel pellets correlate with higher stored energy in Wendelstein 7-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17751). Analysis of W7-X pellet-injection experiments from 2024 and 2025 finds a positive correlation between hydrogen pellet size and quasi-steady-state stored plasma energy. Pellets deposited sufficiently deep create density gradients that suppress ion-temperature-gradient turbulence and improve confinement. The measured deposition position also differs from the standard neutral-gas-shielding model, suggesting substantial inward transport of the pellet cloud. Larger pellets may offer another route to stronger long-pulse performance.

[Independent teams produce counterexamples to Grad’s 59-year-old conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26742). Matt Landreman constructs exact, non-axisymmetric plasma equilibria with nested toroidal magnetic surfaces and smooth pressure and current distributions; an independent paper posted a day earlier produces another family of counterexamples. Landreman says two of his solution families were discovered using GPT-6 Astra. Besides settling an old mathematical question, the exact solutions provide useful benchmarks for three-dimensional equilibrium codes.

[Disruption-prediction study finds that the useful warning window matters more than detailed timing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24443). The study compares machine-learning alarm methods using DIII-D, EAST, and Alcator C-Mod data. Directly predicting disruption probability within an actionable time window gives the best average alarm performance on DIII-D and EAST. All tested methods perform poorly on C-Mod, so this is not a universal disruption predictor.

[TCV experiments connect magnetic-drift direction with edge electric fields and access to H-mode](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31424). Reversing the ion magnetic-drift direction changes the edge electric-field structure even when density and temperature profiles are nearly identical. The favorable configuration develops stronger edge-flow shear as heating approaches the high-confinement transition, reinforcing observations from other tokamaks.

[Simpler edge-plasma models outperform more elaborate fits in a TCV comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27435). Researchers calibrate several SOLPS-ITER transport models against TCV measurements, then test them with the magnetic field reversed. Adding adjustable parameters improved the original fit but worsened predictive performance; the simplest constant-diffusivity model performed best overall. A useful reminder that matching yesterday’s experiment is not the same as predicting tomorrow’s.

[PQLS adds uncertainty estimates to fast plasma-transport predictions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29336). The quasilinear transport solver includes electromagnetic and collisional effects and uses Bayesian calibration to distinguish uncertainty in fitted parameters from limitations of the model itself. Parameter uncertainty is propagated into predicted plasma profiles and compared with more computationally expensive gyrokinetic simulations.

[A probabilistic AI model captures multiple possible divertor-plasma states](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28116). The surrogate for SOLPS-ITER simulations preserves mesh geometry and predicts distributions rather than a single answer. Near sensitive operating transitions, it captures separate hot and cold plasma states instead of averaging them into a potentially misleading intermediate state.

[KSTAR identifies magnetic error fields in one high-confinement plasma discharge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30452). Researchers report a non-disruptive calibration technique that reduces the required number of discharges from four to one, potentially cutting calibration time by up to 75%. The work extends an approach previously demonstrated in lower-confinement plasmas toward conditions relevant to ITER and fusion pilot plants.

[A proposed ITER correction scheme tackles both resonant and nonresonant magnetic errors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29849). The method targets residual magnetic perturbations that can remain after conventional resonant-error correction. In modeled ITER scenarios, it reduces unwanted torque and resonant response, outperforming single-mode correction in most cases and approaching more elaborate multimode correction. These are calculations for ITER, not results from operating it.

[Researchers design sub-10-microsecond neural-network inference for tokamak control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23141). A compressed recurrent probabilistic neural network targeting programmable FPGA hardware fits the hardware’s resource limits and delivers single-step inference below 10 microseconds. That establishes a computing capability for real-time plasma-state prediction, not yet a complete demonstration of improved plasma control.

[LLNL improves predictions of laser backscatter at NIF](https://lasers.llnl.gov/news/llnl-researchers-improve-predictive-modeling-laser-backscatter). Accounting for different plasma species moving through one another substantially improves agreement between simulations and measured backscatter. Simply increasing simulation resolution had not fixed the discrepancy. Better physics, rather than just more computing, was needed.

[Blue Laser Fusion paper proposes a way to suppress cross-beam energy transfer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-026-02829-8). The Blue Laser Fusion and University of Rochester team finds that tuning the frequency and polarization of crossing laser beams can produce a slowly rotating polarization state that suppresses cross-beam energy transfer, potentially reducing wasted laser energy without additional hardware. The *Communications Physics* paper was published August 22, although Blue Laser Fusion highlighted it this fortnight.

[Low-density radiofrequency heating exposes limitations of cold-plasma modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30155). The work examines the low-edge-density regime pursued to reduce plasma-wall interactions during ion-cyclotron heating. Including collisions removes mathematical singularities but leaves extremely narrow electric-field peaks. The authors conclude that collisions do not eliminate the need to resolve the small spatial scales ordinarily associated with more complete plasma physics.

[Titanium x-ray spectra offer another way to diagnose laser-fusion energy losses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18423). Atomic-physics modeling is used to infer energetic-electron populations associated with stimulated Raman scattering, which can divert laser energy and preheat the fuel. Reanalysis of historical Nova laser measurements agrees better with independently measured scattering losses, suggesting a complement to conventional backscatter diagnostics—not a new fusion-yield result.
