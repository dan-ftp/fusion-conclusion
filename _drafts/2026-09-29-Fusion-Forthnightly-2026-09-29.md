## Companies, financing, and commercial development

[ENN reports proton-boron fusion reactions on Xuanlong-50U](https://www.eeo.com.cn/2026/0928/1050796.shtml) and [begins construction on Helong-2](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/china-begins-building-hydrogen-boron-fusion-device?utm_source=chatgpt.com); both machines are spherical tokamaks. ENN says Xuanlong-50U produced more than 10⁸ proton-boron fusion reactions/s (about 0.14 mW of fusion power, a 1 GW fusion source would require roughly 7×10²⁰ p-B reactions/s), claiming to be the first to demonstrate p-B fusion on a commercial fusion company’s own machine. For comparison, the recent p-B results on LHD were about 10¹⁴ reactions/s. Helong-2 broke ground September 15 and is intended to pursue electricity generation by 2030, on a similar time-scale to what TAE proposes. As with TAE, the large gap in present performance and what's needed for a power plant gives the date low credibility.

[First Light Fusion reports compression results for FLARE](https://firstlightfusion.com/media/first-light-fusion-achieves-major-step-forward-for-flare-proving-key-low-cost-fusion-principle/). Experiments on M3 tested multi-shell targets designed to control fuel compression using a simpler, lower-power electrical driver. The company explicitly says these experiments were not designed to demonstrate ignition or fusion gain. Combining compression with the separate rapid-heating stage remains ahead.

[Type One Energy completes the first phase of DOE’s Milestone-Based Fusion Development Program](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/type-one-energy-has-completed-the-first-phase-ugcPost-7508564481640165376-hp4i/). Type One says it completed all seven science, technology, and commercial milestones in its first budget period, spanning HTS magnet testing through development of the technology roadmap for its Infinity Two power plant. The milestones are independently reviewed, although the underlying quantitative performance metrics are generally protected.

[EnableFusion closes a ₩45 billion Series A](https://fusionxinvest.com/news/13016/enablefusion-closes-%E2%82%A945bn-series-a/). Quantum Ventures Korea led the round, which brings total funding to approximately ₩56 billion ($42 million). The Korean company connects fusion developers with manufacturing suppliers and is also expanding into HTS, fusion AI, and tritium-fuel capabilities. Selling the capability to build fusion machines rather than just another design for one.

[StandardX raises £10 million to produce medical isotopes and, eventually, tritium](https://www.standardx.tech/news-stories/standardx-launches-with-ps10m-seed-round-to-create-the-rare-isotopes-needed-to-advance-medical-and-fusion-innovation). Vsquared Ventures and East X Ventures led the seed round for the accelerator-based isotope company. Initial medical-isotope deliveries are planned for 2027, with tritium coming later. StandardX says it already has agreements with unnamed fusion developers. Medical customers provide a business that need not wait for fusion power.

[Daedal Systems raises $4.04 million for plasma diagnostics](https://www.daedalsystems.com/funding-announcement). OMERS Ventures led the round, which will fund the company’s first three diagnostic systems and deployments with pilot customers. Founder Henry Gould previously built diagnostics at General Fusion. The proposition is straightforward: fusion companies should not all have to build the same measurement equipment themselves.

[Marvel Fusion launches Caelora to commercialize high-energy lasers](https://www.marvelfusion.com/newsroom/marvel-fusion-launches-caelora). The wholly owned subsidiary will develop diode-pumped lasers for fusion and scientific research, plus radiation sources for medical, industrial, and security applications. It builds on technology developed with Pulsed Light Technologies under SPRIND funding. Another fusion developer pursuing revenue from its enabling technology before selling electricity.

[nT-Tao and Orion Nuclear agree to explore U.S. fusion deployments](https://www.utilitydive.com/press-release/20260921-nt-tao-and-orion-nuclear-energy-corporation-announce-strategic-collaboratio/). Orion will look for customers, sites, financing, and regulatory pathways for nT-Tao’s proposed systems, designed to supply up to 20 MW of electricity to data centers and industrial facilities. This is a development MoU, not a power purchase agreement, and the fusion system remains under development.

## Supply chain, manufacturing, and enabling technology

[SPARC’s cryogenic system reaches 8 kelvin](https://blog.cfs.energy/cryogenics-how-well-make-our-fusion-machine-magnets-colder-than-pluto/). CFS reports that its cryogenic system has reached its operating temperature in a controlled cooldown. A bypass allows helium circulation and commissioning while tokamak construction continues, so this is not yet a cooldown of the completed magnet system. Integrated support-system tests and subsequent testing with the tokamak remain ahead.

[Inertia partners with Coherent, Laserline, and Leonardo to industrialize its laser supply chain](https://inertia.com/press/inertia-announces-collaborations-with-semiconductor-industry-leaders). The collaborations cover semiconductor lasers, pump systems, cooling, and manufacturing. Inertia estimates that its proposed 10 MJ power-plant laser will require approximately 100 million diodes and that production must increase by roughly two orders of magnitude. The laser is also a substantial factory-building problem.

[ITER’s final vacuum-vessel sector reaches France](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/last-iter-vacuum-vessel-sector-travelling). The ninth and final sector arrived in Marseille on September 21, with delivery to the ITER site expected in early October. Manufactured by the Ansaldo Nucleare–Westinghouse–Walter Tosto consortium, it completes Europe’s five-sector contribution; Korea supplied the other four. A substantial procurement milestone, though delivery is not the same as completing vessel assembly.

[ITER’s tritium plant moves from design into procurement and installation](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/teams-around-world-meet-tritium-plant-contracts-advance). ITER reports that all tritium-plant systems have reached final-design status, with initial tanks and piping already being fabricated and installed. Korea is advancing storage and delivery systems, the United States exhaust processing, and Japan the central detritiation plant. Major equipment deliveries for the next phase are scheduled for 2028.

[Renaissance Fusion, DIFFER, and VDL agree to develop a Dutch fusion manufacturing partnership](https://www.differ.nl/news/renaissancefusion-vdl-differ). The agreement combines Renaissance’s fuel-cycle and blanket technologies, DIFFER’s plasma-facing materials and liquid-metal expertise, and VDL’s precision manufacturing capabilities. The partners envisage joint development followed by an industrial consortium. A proposed high-field stellarator demonstrator remains contingent on technical progress and financing.

[Fusion for Energy publishes six reports mapping fusion technology and supply-chain gaps](https://fusionforenergy.europa.eu/news/fusion-technology-mapping-reports/). The reports cover fuel cycles, magnets, additive manufacturing, plasma-facing components, metrology and reverse engineering, and artificial intelligence. They identify European capabilities, supply bottlenecks, and dependence on external suppliers. F4E also expects to sign nine technology-development contracts in the coming months—useful reading for suppliers looking for problems someone may actually pay them to solve.

[Missouri’s research reactor will test graphite for ITER diagnostics](https://showme.missouri.edu/2026/mizzou-plays-critical-role-in-one-of-the-worlds-biggest-energy-projects/). The University of Missouri Research Reactor will irradiate graphite used in a PPPL-designed instrument for measuring plasma-particle temperatures. The tests will assess radiation-induced degradation before deployment at ITER. Existing fission infrastructure doing useful qualification work for fusion hardware.

## Government, regulation, and public programs

[Bipartisan House bill proposes $10 billion for fusion commercialization](https://democrats-science.house.gov/news/press-releases/lofgren-and-obernolte-lead-introduction-of-american-leadership-in-fusion-act). The American Leadership in Fusion Act would provide $10 billion in direct investments, establish an Office of Fusion at DOE, and support research infrastructure, commercialization, and workforce development. This is proposed legislation, not an enacted funding package.

[China lays out HL-3 and HL-4 development plans and expands international fusion cooperation](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/china-sets-out-fusion-progress-collaboration-opportunities). China Fusion Energy says HL-3 is in the final phase of upgrades for planned D-T burning-plasma physics experiments in 2027, while construction has begun on the Shanghai Fusion Science Research Centre that is expected to host the proposed HL-4 device and finish in 2030. A 25 T high-field magnet development and test line is planned for 2028, with a prototype magnet targeted for 2030. The same IAEA-side event produced a new ITER cooperation agreement and a proposal for a global fusion supply-chain sharing program. These are program and construction milestones, not new plasma-performance results.

[South Korea plans central oversight for fusion and six other strategic technologies](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/09/20/deputy-pm-to-oversee-new-team-for-koreas-seven-seed-projects). The science ministry plans a dedicated unit reporting to the deputy prime minister to coordinate its seven SEED programs, including fusion. It will conduct regular reviews of roadmaps, progress, and obstacles alongside efforts to remove regulatory barriers. The new development is the coordinating machinery; the underlying technology program was announced in August.

[Helical Fusion joins a government-backed study of fusion research facilities in Kawasaki](https://www.helicalfusion.com/post/helical-fusion%E3%80%81%E5%B7%9D%E5%B4%8E%E5%B8%82%E3%81%A7gx%E7%94%A3%E6%A5%AD%E6%8B%A0%E7%82%B9%E5%BD%A2%E6%88%90%E3%81%AB%E5%90%91%E3%81%91%E3%81%9Ffs%E3%83%BBpre-feed%E8%AA%BF%E6%9F%BB%E4%BA%8B%E6%A5%AD%E3%81%AB%E5%8F%82%E7%94%BB). A METI-supported feasibility and preliminary engineering study will examine requirements for future fusion research and demonstration facilities, including reuse of existing industrial infrastructure in Kawasaki’s Minamiwatarida district. The broader industrial-hub study runs through February 2027. This supports facilities planning, not a commitment to construct a fusion power plant.

## People and hiring

[Daedal Systems hires Alon Hazan as chief technology and product officer](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/whenrygould_im-excited-to-welcome-alon-hazan-as-daedal-share-7509269771519991809-8po1/). Hazan was previously CTO of RetiSpec, where he helped move hyperspectral retinal-scanning technology from a benchtop prototype into an FDA clinical trial. He will lead industrial development of Daedal’s plasma-diagnostic products—adding a commercialization executive just after the company raised its first institutional round.

[Fusion Science and Technology publishes a special issue on growing the fusion workforce](https://wx1.ans.org/pubs/journals/fst/v_82). The NSF-funded collection grew out of the Workforce Accelerator for Fusion Energy Development Conference and covers apprenticeships and certification, university programs, industry-driven curricula, inertial-fusion workforce development, policy, and pathways into fusion careers. The issue itself was published in August, but the papers have been resurfacing this fortnight.

## Legal and intellectual property

[New retrospective details how IP disputes slowed DOE’s fusion milestone program](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.28398v1). Three former DOE officials describe months of stalled negotiations over government patent and data rights. DOE ultimately narrowed those rights and replaced march-in rights with conditional licenses, but companies still reduced or dropped technical milestones to avoid IP entanglements. Not quite the intended effect of a program meant to accelerate commercialization.

[American Fusion says it now has 151 pending U.S. patent applications](https://americanfusionenergy.com/news/american-fusion-inc-otcqb-amfn-provides-corporate-update-on-intellectual-property-texatron-testing-and-national-exchange-uplisting/). Up from the 130 reported last fortnight: the company announced another 21 applications on September 22, covering variations in Texatron’s materials, geometry, and components. These remain applications, not granted patents.

[Tibbar Plasma Technologies’ advanced-fuel fusion-reactor patent application is published](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20260290629). The application describes oscillating plasma rings that repeatedly collide, including hydrogen-boron configurations, with direct conversion of charged fusion products into electricity. The filing acknowledges NSF funding and government rights in the invention. This is a newly published application, not a patent grant.

## Papers and research

[LLNL and Inertia publish target designs projecting fusion gains of 26–43](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19752). The preprint describes indirect-drive targets predicted to produce 265–427 MJ of fusion energy from a 10 MJ laser pulse. The designs scale up NIF’s approach while addressing manufacturing imperfections and power-plant requirements. These are simulated target gains, not demonstrated performance or net electricity.

[Tritium-lean startup could substantially reduce the breeding requirements for early fusion plants](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352179126001262). Niek Lopes Cardozo and Erin van Rheenen find that relying on each generation of fusion plants to breed startup tritium for the next would require impractically high overbreeding. They instead propose intermittent, tritium-lean startup that could bring a plant to full operation within months with much less initial tritium. If the model holds up, maximizing blanket TBR may be less important than commonly assumed.

[AI brings turbulent transport directly into stellarator design optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28730). A surrogate trained on more than 200,000 gyrokinetic simulations spanning about 23,000 stellarator configurations makes millisecond transport predictions. That lets researchers optimize turbulent heat losses across the plasma rather than rely on indirect proxies. The reported improvements remain computational, with a simplified electron model.

[Reactor-scale stellarator study finds neutron wall loading sets the size floor](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1741-4326/aea918). A *Nuclear Fusion* systems study couples a SQuID stellarator design to the PROCESS plant model and finds that neutron wall loading, rather than magnetic field, becomes the main constraint on shrinking an HTS machine. Even with an optimistic 1.5 MW/m² wall-load limit, the modeled 1 GWe plant has a 16.5 m major radius. HTS beats low-temperature superconductors economically only if its material costs remain below roughly five times LTS costs, assuming comparable manufacturing complexity and risk. Better magnets do not make the rest of the plant disappear.

[Larger fuel pellets correlate with higher stored energy in Wendelstein 7-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17751). Analysis of W7-X pellet-injection experiments from 2024 and 2025 finds a positive correlation between hydrogen pellet size and quasi-steady-state stored plasma energy. Pellets deposited sufficiently deep create density gradients that suppress ion-temperature-gradient turbulence and improve confinement. The measured deposition position also differs from the standard neutral-gas-shielding model, suggesting substantial inward transport of the pellet cloud. Larger pellets may offer another route to stronger long-pulse performance.

[Independent teams produce counterexamples to Grad’s 59-year-old conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26742). Matt Landreman constructs exact, non-axisymmetric plasma equilibria with nested toroidal magnetic surfaces and smooth pressure and current distributions; an independent paper posted a day earlier produces another family of counterexamples. Landreman says two of his solution families were discovered using GPT-6 Astra. Besides settling an old mathematical question, the exact solutions provide useful benchmarks for three-dimensional equilibrium codes.

[Disruption-prediction study finds that the useful warning window matters more than detailed timing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24443). The study compares machine-learning alarm methods using DIII-D, EAST, and Alcator C-Mod data. Directly predicting disruption probability within an actionable time window gives the best average alarm performance on DIII-D and EAST. All tested methods perform poorly on C-Mod, so this is not a universal disruption predictor.

[TCV experiments connect magnetic-drift direction with edge electric fields and access to H-mode](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31424). Reversing the ion magnetic-drift direction changes the edge electric-field structure even when density and temperature profiles are nearly identical. The favorable configuration develops stronger edge-flow shear as heating approaches the high-confinement transition, reinforcing observations from other tokamaks.

[Simpler edge-plasma models outperform more elaborate fits in a TCV comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27435). Researchers calibrate several SOLPS-ITER transport models against TCV measurements, then test them with the magnetic field reversed. Adding adjustable parameters improved the original fit but worsened predictive performance; the simplest constant-diffusivity model performed best overall. A useful reminder that matching yesterday’s experiment is not the same as predicting tomorrow’s.

[PQLS adds uncertainty estimates to fast plasma-transport predictions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29336). The quasilinear transport solver includes electromagnetic and collisional effects and uses Bayesian calibration to distinguish uncertainty in fitted parameters from limitations of the model itself. Parameter uncertainty is propagated into predicted plasma profiles and compared with more computationally expensive gyrokinetic simulations.

[A probabilistic AI model captures multiple possible divertor-plasma states](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28116). The surrogate for SOLPS-ITER simulations preserves mesh geometry and predicts distributions rather than a single answer. Near sensitive operating transitions, it captures separate hot and cold plasma states instead of averaging them into a potentially misleading intermediate state.

[KSTAR identifies magnetic error fields in one high-confinement plasma discharge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30452). Researchers report a non-disruptive calibration technique that reduces the required number of discharges from four to one, potentially cutting calibration time by up to 75%. The work extends an approach previously demonstrated in lower-confinement plasmas toward conditions relevant to ITER and fusion pilot plants.

[A proposed ITER correction scheme tackles both resonant and nonresonant magnetic errors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29849). The method targets residual magnetic perturbations that can remain after conventional resonant-error correction. In modeled ITER scenarios, it reduces unwanted torque and resonant response, outperforming single-mode correction in most cases and approaching more elaborate multimode correction. These are calculations for ITER, not results from operating it.

[Researchers design sub-10-microsecond neural-network inference for tokamak control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23141). A compressed recurrent probabilistic neural network targeting programmable FPGA hardware fits the hardware’s resource limits and delivers single-step inference below 10 microseconds. That establishes a computing capability for real-time plasma-state prediction, not yet a complete demonstration of improved plasma control.

[LLNL improves predictions of laser backscatter at NIF](https://lasers.llnl.gov/news/llnl-researchers-improve-predictive-modeling-laser-backscatter). Accounting for different plasma species moving through one another substantially improves agreement between simulations and measured backscatter. Simply increasing simulation resolution had not fixed the discrepancy. Better physics, rather than just more computing, was needed.

[Blue Laser Fusion paper proposes a way to suppress cross-beam energy transfer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-026-02829-8). The Blue Laser Fusion and University of Rochester team finds that tuning the frequency and polarization of crossing laser beams can produce a slowly rotating polarization state that suppresses cross-beam energy transfer, potentially reducing wasted laser energy without additional hardware. The *Communications Physics* paper was published August 22, although Blue Laser Fusion highlighted it this fortnight.

[Low-density radiofrequency heating exposes limitations of cold-plasma modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30155). The work examines the low-edge-density regime pursued to reduce plasma-wall interactions during ion-cyclotron heating. Including collisions removes mathematical singularities but leaves extremely narrow electric-field peaks. The authors conclude that collisions do not eliminate the need to resolve the small spatial scales ordinarily associated with more complete plasma physics.

[Titanium x-ray spectra offer another way to diagnose laser-fusion energy losses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18423). Atomic-physics modeling is used to infer energetic-electron populations associated with stimulated Raman scattering, which can divert laser energy and preheat the fuel. Reanalysis of historical Nova laser measurements agrees better with independently measured scattering losses, suggesting a complement to conventional backscatter diagnostics—not a new fusion-yield result.
