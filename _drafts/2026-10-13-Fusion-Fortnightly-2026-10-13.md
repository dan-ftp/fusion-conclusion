# Fusion Fortnightly | 2026-10-12

No fluff, all facts: Type One Energy raises $200 million. General Fusion reaches 1 keV on LM26. Germany launches a €500 million fusion milestone program. CFS orders more than 10,000 km of superconducting tape for ARC. Helical Fusion raises another ¥3.23 billion. STEP unveils its latest power plant design.

## Companies, financing, and commercial development

[Type One Energy closes a $200 million Series B](https://typeoneenergy.com/insights-announcements/type-one-energy-completes-200m-series-b-financing/). Total investment in Type One now exceeds $400 million. The funding supports Infinity One, its engineering prototype in Tennessee, but certainly isn't enough to get to first plasma.

[General Fusion reports LM26 plasma temperatures above 1 keV](https://generalfusion.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Thomson-Scattering-Measurement-of-1-keV-Electron-Temperature-on-Lawson-Machine-26.pdf). Lawson Machine 26 achieved electron temperatures exceeding 1 keV (12 million °C) through compression, with meaningful ion heating also reported. The results were co-published with UKAEA and measured using Thomson scattering diagnostics. This is a significant intermediate milestone for General Fusion's magnetized target fusion approach, though still short of its next goals of 10 keV and satisfying the Lawson criterion. It does not establish fusion breakeven or net energy production.

[General Fusion measures electron temperatures above 1 keV on LM26](https://generalfusion.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Thomson-Scattering-Measurement-of-1-keV-Electron-Temperature-on-Lawson-Machine-26.pdf). Thomson scattering measured a peak local electron temperature of 1.09 keV, up from 0.72 keV reported in July, aided by a new diagnostic view closer to the plasma core. A [companion paper](https://generalfusion.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Compressional-heating-above-1-keV-on-the-LM26-magnetized-target-fusion-machine.pdf) reports a later peak of 1.18 keV from AXUV measurements and neutron-inferred ion heating from 323 eV to 459 eV. The improved performance is attributed to better electron energy confinement, although plasma temperature still collapses at a compression ratio of 2.7. See the [Fusion Conclusion's July claim check](https://www.fusionconclusion.com/Claim-Check-2026-07-02/) for analysis of the earlier results. An interesting first data point for public market reactions to company-defined milestones: [GFUZ](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/GFUZ/?guccounter=1&guce_referrer=aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZ29vZ2xlLmNvbS8&guce_referrer_sig=AQAAAEzLBq6SmJDjFniMHH97hw_68ThzDEEi4pHutBcCuQlZjMGM-1VlBB3Hf6TEAlbW_akNGsZHJqeFCS4uDbKjFDHEZa_eeM5zihv4KbYwGa9IO1f7rjbb_YLSq7iHY4a09RFZRyoP2LU4aOrLFN5uLrk5sOp3AdAV2OUwwg1rN0qv) trading volume increased 5-10 times the previous weeks and the price transiently went up about 10% but is still well within the scatter of the last month or so. It looks like the news of 1 keV electrons created interest but not value for the company.

[Helical Fusion raises another ¥3.23 billion ($20.6 million) in a Series B second close](https://global.helicalfusion.com/post/fuji-electric-joins-helical-fusion-as-strategic-investor-and-official-partner-as-series-b-second-clo). The company reports approximately ¥13.15 billion ($84 million) in cumulative funding, including grants and loans. 

[Xcimer secures up to $30.5 million more from DOE's fusion milestone program](https://xcimer.energy/news/xcimer-energy-receives-additional-30-5-million-award-from-doe-milestone-program/). No info on what the new milestones encompase.

[Zenithon AI raises $10 million to develop AI models for extreme physics](https://www.kings.cam.ac.uk/news/start-backed-kings-e-lab-raises-10m). Founded by [Alex Higginbottom](https://www.linkedin.com/in/alex-higginbottom-049056141/) and [Abetharan Antony](https://www.linkedin.com/in/abetharan-antony/), the company is building physics-based AI world models for fusion reactors, rockets, and semiconductor manufacturing.

[The 1 Cent Fusion Energy project concludes, and its founders move on to Borealis Fusion](https://1cf.energy/1cfe-completed/). A major conclusion that the non-fusion core components alone, under their assumptions, would cost 2.9 cents/kWh. Availability and financing were among the largest cost drivers. Fusion fuel was also important because of the impacts of what blanket and breeding systems are needed. They've made their [source code](https://github.com/1cFE) available on github. After doing this study, two members are going on to found [Borealis Fusion](https://borealisfusion.com/) to do p-B11 fusion. 

## Supply chain, manufacturing, and enabling technology

[CFS places an order for more than 10,000 kilometers of HTS tape with Fujikura](https://www.fujikura.co.jp/en/news/pressrelease/20260930cfsarc1km.html). The long-term supply agreement supports development of ARC fusion power plants, beginning with the proposed Fall Line Fusion Power Station in Virginia. Fujikura says it is the largest single HTS tape order ever placed and will expand manufacturing capacity to deliver it. The order represents 3 billion ampere-meters of superconductor, a substantial step from experimental magnet procurement to commercial-plant supply. The contract value was not disclosed.

[Avalanche Energy selects Washington's Tri-Cities region for its first fusion-machine manufacturing facility](https://www.avalanchefusion.com/news-release/avalanche-energy-and-tridec-advance-plans-for-first-global-fusion-machine-manufacturing-facility-in-the-tri-cities). Avalanche and the Tri-City Development Council (TRIDEC) signed a strategic engagement agreement covering site selection, permitting, infrastructure, workforce development, and local supply chains. The company intends to begin with prototype production and ultimately manufacture its compact fusion machines at commercial scale, potentially creating hundreds of jobs. This is a facility-development agreement, not yet a commitment to build or an operating factory.

[DIII-D and Kyoto Fusioneering begin developing a divertor for negative-triangularity plasmas](https://kyotofusioneering.com/en/news/2026/10/08/4758). The collaboration will design, manufacture, install, and experimentally test a new divertor optimized for negative-triangularity operation. Kyoto Fusioneering will manufacture the plasma-facing tiles. Negative triangularity has demonstrated attractive confinement and stability properties, but reactor-relevant exhaust and particle control remain open engineering questions. The project is intended to test those issues on DIII-D, with resulting data available to the broader research program.

[Larmor mmWave Systems introduces its high-power microwave business](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/larmor_the-fusion-industry-needs-a-spark-plug-activity-7511450675839442944-5jGC). The EPFL Swiss Plasma Center spinout, co-founded by Massimo Carpita and Mackenzie van Rossem, aims to commercialize high-power, high-frequency microwave systems for fusion plasma heating and control. The company's approach is to turn traditionally customized scientific equipment into more standardized industrial products that can serve multiple fusion developers and other markets.

[Kyoto Fusioneering describes its in-house gyrotron simulation code](https://kyotofusioneering.com/en/news/2026/09/30/4698). Engineer Satoru Kobayashi developed a code that models electron-beam behavior through a gyrotron, including space-charge effects and microwave generation. Kyoto Fusioneering says it reduces calculations that previously took hours to about five minutes. Faster and more accurate design tools should help narrow the gap between gyrotron simulations and experimental performance.

## Government, regulation, and public programs

[Germany launches a €500 million milestone-based fusion development program](https://www.sprind.org/en/words/magazine/nuclear-fusion-milestone-program). The Federal Agency for Breakthrough Innovation (SPRIND) will administer the program on behalf of the German government, with €500 million to be made available by 2029 and the possibility of up to €2.5 billion in longer-term public funding. Multiple companies will compete for support tied to technical and financial milestones, with independent evaluations and funding terminated when milestones are missed. The program can employ equity, grants, loans, and other instruments, giving the government a potential share of the economic upside. Germany is adopting a model similar in spirit to the U.S. DOE fusion milestone program, but with a much larger funding envelope.

[STEP Fusion unveils its third-generation prototype power plant design, SPP3](https://stepfusion.com/step-fusion-unveils-next-generation-fusion-powerplant-design-at-soft-2026/). Presented at SOFT 2026, the latest design emphasizes steady-state operation, improved gyrotron efficiency, maintainability, and plant availability. New features include a vertical maintenance strategy and a stacked-rings architecture intended to simplify replacement of in-vessel components. The program continues to target net electrical production, tritium fuel self-sufficiency, and a design that industrial suppliers can reproduce for subsequent power plants. These remain design objectives, not demonstrated operating capabilities.

[The U.S.-Japan Technology Prosperity Deal highlights Kyoto Fusioneering's partnership with ORNL](https://kyotofusioneering.com/en/news/2026/10/08/4756). The joint government statement specifically identifies the UNITY-3 breeding-blanket test facility planned for Oak Ridge National Laboratory in Tennessee and access for U.S. researchers to Kyoto Fusioneering's UNITY-1 facility in Japan. UNITY-3 is intended to use an accelerator-driven D-T neutron source to test tritium-breeding blanket mockups. The statement also highlights joint work on JT-60SA and negative-triangularity research at DIII-D. The underlying KF-ORNL partnership was previously announced; its inclusion in the bilateral framework is the new development.

[ITER receives its final major core tokamak component](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/remarkable-chapter-draws-close). Vacuum-vessel sector No. 2 arrived at the ITER site on October 2, completing delivery of all nine vacuum-vessel sectors and the major components required for the tokamak core, including its magnets and thermal shields. It closes a significant international manufacturing and procurement phase. Considerable assembly and integration work remains before the machine can operate.

[CFS and PPPL join a new phase of DOE's Genesis Mission to build a SPARC digital twin](https://www.pppl.gov/news/2026/pppl-data-simulation-codes-and-compute-power-will-be-used-phase-ii-genesis-mission). CFS will lead the Phase II project, bringing together four DOE national laboratories, universities, and private partners to develop a digital twin connected to the SPARC tokamak. PPPL will contribute experimental data, physics codes including TRANSP, XGC, and M3D-C1, and high-performance computing resources. The project targets full-pulse predictions in less than 20 minutes, fast enough to inform decisions between experimental pulses, with the broader goal of improving SPARC operation and subsequent ARC design.

## Papers and research

[The 1 Cent Fusion Energy project publishes its final comparative economics analysis](https://1cf.energy/1cfe-completed/). The Astera-supported effort produced the open-source 1costingFE framework, covering four fusion fuels and 17 confinement concepts, and an automated analysis of 38 modeled concepts. Its conclusion is that fusion electricity at 1¢/kWh is unlikely under ordinary commercial assumptions, although not impossible in favorable speculative cases.

The most interesting results concern the costs outside the fusion core. In an illustrative 1 GWe D-T plant with the core assigned zero capital cost, the modeled electricity cost was still about 2.9¢/kWh, assuming 85% availability, a 7% cost of capital, six-year construction, and a 30-year plant lifetime. Availability was the most sensitive variable in 29 of 38 cases, while financing cost ranked among the five most sensitive in 33. The work highlights how uptime, financing, and non-core plant costs can overwhelm improvements in plasma performance. Among more speculative low-cost routes, the study highlights D-He3 with direct conversion and p-B11 in a simple mirror. These are modeled outcomes dependent on unproven physics and engineering assumptions, not measured electricity costs.

[IGNITE Tokamak World Model Architecture proposes a generative AI model for plasma evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02515). The preprint describes a foundation-model architecture trained on more than a decade of DIII-D experimental data. It aims to predict complete tokamak discharges from initial plasma conditions and actuator trajectories, with the ability to generate trajectories from desired outcomes or text prompts. The approach could support faster experiment planning and eventually real-time control. Predictive accuracy outside its training data, rare-event behavior, and transfer between machines remain important questions.

[Professional Perspectives on Community and Public Engagement with Fusion Energy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02546) examines how people working in the U.S. fusion industry view engagement with local communities and the public. The researchers found that fusion professionals generally see value in public engagement but want greater organizational and expert support. The paper argues for moving beyond one-way science communication toward more direct public participation. This will become more important as commercial projects advance through siting, permitting, construction, and operation.
