## Companies, financing, and commercial development

[Helion and CFS to receive up to $260 million in CHIPS Act funding](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/10/department-commerce-announces-letters-intent-4-companies-389-million-rd). The U.S. Department of Commerce signed letters of intent to provide up to $175 million to Helion and $85 million to CFS, together accounting for two-thirds of a $389 million round of semiconductor R&D funding. Helion will continue to develop high-voltage equipment for its pulsed-power systems. The project also includes $50 million in private investment. CFS will develop power electronics, RF heating generators, fast plasma controllers, radiation-hardened electronics, and AI-related hardware for future ARC plants. CHIPS Act is funding these because it is within its mandate for the development of advanced semiconductors and domestic manufacturing capabilities.

A notable bit is that the federal government will receive minority, non-voting equity stakes in both companies in exchange for the funding. For Helion, the government would receive a $175 million equity interest valued at the company's most recent Series G financing; the terms for CFS have not been disclosed (I welcome my American taxpayer readers for joining me as new owners in CFS!). Specific technical milestones and payment conditions are not public. There's been some noise about this announcement around the administration in supporting Helion unfairly supporting family investments (by way of Kushner at Thrive Capital), which I find a bit overblown. As TAE is merging with Trump Media and Technology Group and has it's own power electronics business that is not being supported by CHIPS funding. In my opinion, the pulsed power work at Helion is one of the few things they are doing that is of genuine value. 

[Marathon Fusion reports on its first production of gold from mercury using fusion neutrons](https://x.com/MarathonFusion/status/2113001073019662481), improving a CERN benchmark by 10x. It also claims a new plasma-centrifuge isotope-enrichment milestone. 

[Hephaestus raises $17.2 million to make rare-earth-free HTS and negative triangularity tokamaks](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261020291741/en/). The company plans to start manufacturing the new HTS materials at a 70,000-square-foot facility in New Jersey in 2027 (starting I can see happening by then but not major production) and targets a Q>1 reactor demonstration by 2030 (essentially impossible). They claim to have discovered and fabricated novel rare-earth-free HTS materials using AI, with laboratory characterization by the UK's National Physical Laboratory. It has not disclosed any important information for assessing performance relevance for use in fusion devices. Rare-earth-free HTS materials already exist (e.g., Bi-2212) but matching REBCO's high-field performance and manufacturing scalability has not yet been met. The rare earth costs for HTS don't matter: 10,000 km of 4 mm REBCO tape (how much CFS recently announced purchasing that supplies ~1/2 and ARC) contains only around 80 kg of yttrium, only a few thousand dollars. Chinese export restrictions pose a procurement risk (see: [Japan faces a potential yttrium supply squeeze as Chinese export restrictions tighten](https://www.ft.com/content/ebd3244a-9120-4773-b55f-41df77f60c93)), but the much larger challenge for fusion is manufacturing sufficient quantities of affordable, high-quality HTS tape. Until we get quantitative performance data, it is impossible to assess whether Hephaestus has made a meaningful technical advance.

[Cambridge Heavy Industries raises $6.2 million for compact laser fusion](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/22/3386302/0/en/cambridge-heavy-industries-raises-6-2-million-seed-round-to-advance-its-modular-benchtop-scale-fusion-system-technology.html). The startup spun out of MIT raised a seed round led by Azolla Ventures. CHI says its approach can generate extreme target pressure with relatively small amounts of laser energy and has reported an approximately 350 GPa compression result using tens of millijoules (remember it needs to get both dense and hot enough for doing fusion). It is exploring tritium production, neutron imaging, and materials testing as intermediate markets. 

[Gauss Fusion closes its Seed-to-A round and opens a Series A](https://gauss-fusion.com/news/gauss-fusion-closes-seed-to-a-financing-launches-series-a-fundraising). No information on how much was raised.

[Starlight Engine signs strategic investment agreement](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000014.000152790.html) with the TAKANAWA GATEWAY Global Co-Benefits Fund, which is associated with a major rail and infrastructure company. The investment amount was not disclosed. 

[Helical Fusion agrees to a proposed investment through an individual-investor fund](https://www.helicalfusion.com/post/helical-fusion%E3%80%81%E5%80%8B%E4%BA%BA%E6%8A%95%E8%B3%87%E5%AE%B6%E5%90%91%E3%81%91-%E5%80%8B%E5%88%A5%E9%8A%98%E6%9F%84%E3%83%95%E3%82%A1%E3%83%B3%E3%83%89%E7%AC%AC%EF%BC%94%E5%BC%BE%E3%81%B8%E3%80%81%E3%82%A4%E3%83%BC%E3%82%AF%E3%83%A9%E3%82%A6%E3%83%89%E3%81%A8%E6%8A%95%E8%B3%87%E4%BA%88%E5%AE%9A%E3%81%AB%E9%96%A2%E3%81%99%E3%82%8B%E5%9F%BA%E6%9C%AC%E5%90%88%E6%84%8F%E6%9B%B8%E3%82%92%E7%B7%A0%E7%B5%90). Japan's Helical Fusion signed a basic agreement with eCloud for an investment through a dedicated single-stock fund serving individual investors. 

[Borealis Fusion launches with $6 million to pursue a proton-boron tandem mirror](https://borealisfusion.com/introducing-borealis-fusion/). The company is developing PRISM, a proton-boron rotating-ion-separation mirror, descended from tandem-mirror confinement. Several founders previously worked on the 1¢/kWh Fusion Energy study and Princeton-associated p–B11 research. 

[First Light Fusion wins a UK defense contract for hypervelocity-impact research](https://firstlightfusion.com/media/first-light-fusion-selected-by-the-uk-defence-science-and-technology-laboratory-dstl-to-deliver-hypervelocity-impact-research-programme/). They are planning on firing steel projectiles into graphite at >3 km/s and measure the response using their gas gun, pulsed power, diagnostics, simulations, and proprietary amplification technology. 

[Stellios Technologies raises $5 million for high-power microwave systems](https://dronelife.com/2026/10/21/stellios-technologies-emerges-from-stealth-with-high-power-microwave-systems/). It is initially for counter-drone and other counter-electronics applications but identifies fusion heating as a potential market.

## Supply chain, manufacturing, and enabling technology

[Fusion for Energy signs first IFMIF-DONES procurement arrangements for an injector and EUROFER steel](https://fusionforenergy.europa.eu/news/europe-signs-its-first-procurement-arrangements-for-ifmif-dones/). The first two European in-kind supply agreements for the fusion-neutron materials-testing facility cover its deuteron-beam injector and 15 tonnes of reduced-activation steel. 

[DOE designates helium a critical material](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-designates-helium-critical-material-strengthen-american-manufacturing). In a few decades maybe fusion energy will be producing much of the helium it needs.

[Fusion for Energy publishes a report on building Europe's fusion supply chain](https://fusionforenergy.europa.eu/news/how-did-f4e-build-the-european-fusion-supply-chain/). F4E has released an industrial analysis of the specialist suppliers, production capabilities, and quality systems developed through almost two decades of European ITER contracts. 

## Government, regulation, and public programs

[UK Fusion Energy's annual report reveals STEP spending, supplier growth, and governance weaknesses](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-fusion-energy-ltd-annual-report-and-accounts-202526/uk-fusion-energy-ltd-annual-report-and-accounts-202526-accessible-version). UK Fusion Energy reports spending £118.8 million in 2025–26, 371 British suppliers, and 80% of expenditures done domestically. 

[Israel proposes three levels of investment in a national fusion strategy](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/nuclear-fusion-strategy). Israel's Ministry of Energy and Infrastructure published options ranging from NIS 50–100 million ($16.5-33 million) over five years for research and training to a NIS 1–2 billion ($330-660 million) national program over seven to ten years that could involve a larger experimental facility. Lasers, RF, power electronics, materials, AI, and robotics are indetified as areas where Israeli industry could contribute to the global fusion supply chain. 

[Shanghai's new industrial plan explicitly backs fusion equipment](https://sheitc.sh.gov.cn/cyfz/20261026/246991fa89514b9db6dcb9afb47a6e7c.html). Shanghai's 2027–29 energy-equipment action plan includes high-temperature-superconducting tokamaks and other experimental fusion-device technologies among its targeted sectors. The plan goes beyond plasma research to cover first-of-a-kind applications, industrial development, standards, financing, and manufacturing. This is a municipal economic-development plan, not a disclosure of a new plasma result or a single national funding round. It is useful evidence that China's fusion programs are building local industrial ecosystems in parallel with their reactor research.

[Virginia radiation advisers warn they have no dedicated resources for fusion oversight](https://townhall.virginia.gov/l/GetFile.cfm?File=Meeting%5C58%5C44056%5CMinutes_VDH_44056_v1.pdf). Draft minutes of the October 7 Radiation Advisory Board meeting report that Virginia's Office of Radiological Health is already spending staff time on fusion-related regulatory issues without dedicated funding. Members questioned whether fees collected from existing licensees should subsidize fusion work and discussed uncertainties around anticipated activities by Commonwealth Fusion Systems. The meeting also described broader staffing and budget strain. The minutes were posted during this reporting period and are an unusual glimpse of a practical licensing bottleneck: state agencies can face real workload long before the first plant is built.

[Max Planck and Columbia establish a $13.3 million fusion research center](https://www.mpg.de/27042432/max-planck-columbia-center). The five-year Max Planck Columbia Center, beginning in 2027, will connect Columbia University with Wendelstein 7-X, ASDEX Upgrade, and German and U.S. plasma research groups. Its program covers stellarator exhaust, tokamak-stellarator hybrids, transients, non-planar superconducting magnets, energetic particles, and edge stability. CFS, Proxima Fusion, Thea Energy, and Type One Energy are among companies expected to be invited into working groups. Carlos Paz-Soldan and Thomas Klinger will co-direct the initiative, which also plans exchanges of graduate students and postdocs. The funding is modest compared with private reactor programs, but shared experimental facilities and verified modeling could have broader value.

[IFMIF-DONES partners approve a 2027 program and revised international governance terms](https://ifmif-dones.es/dones-updates/dones-steering-committee-drives-the-programme-into-a-new-phase-with-the-approval-of-key-frameworks-for-implementati/). The multinational DONES Steering Committee approved its 2027 work program, updates to the multilateral agreement and committee rules, and provisions for admitting additional observers. It also reported that construction activity on the main facility and plant systems in Granada had begun. These steps matter because DONES depends on coordinated national contributions and eventual operation as a neutron-irradiation test facility for fusion materials. They are governance and program-execution milestones, not new neutron-exposure data.

[Legal experts warn that public consultation may be a critical path for fusion permitting](https://www.fusionindustryassociation.org/felex-publishes-discussion-paper-on-public-engagement-and-social-acceptance-of-fusion/). FELEX, a group of specialists in fusion law and regulation, published a comparative discussion of public participation and acceptance. It argues for engagement before formal licensing, clearer information about differing national consultation requirements, and possible community benefits or participation mechanisms. No new law was enacted. The point is that a relatively light radiation-safety regime does not eliminate land-use, environmental, local consent, or procedural obstacles, particularly for proposed first-of-a-kind power stations.

[Japan's science minister visits Kyoto Fusioneering's fuel-cycle and gyrotron work](https://kyotofusioneering.com/en/news/2026/10/26/4936). Science and technology minister Toshiharu Furukawa toured Kyoto Fusioneering's development activities on October 22 and discussed the company's expansion into industrial fuel-cycle, plasma-heating, and related technologies. The company's growing network of test facilities and international sales makes it an example of a Japanese fusion supplier building businesses before electricity-producing reactors exist. The visit is a political and industrial-policy signal, not new financial support or a technical performance result.

[EUROfusion names recipients of its 2026 researcher and engineering grants](https://euro-fusion.org/eurofusion-news/2026-researcher-and-engineering-grants/). EUROfusion announced awards to early-career scientists and engineers working at member institutions, supporting research and technical training related to Europe's fusion roadmap. Such programs have a much smaller budget than reactor construction, but they address a real supply constraint in specialized engineering and experimental skills. The announcement identifies recipients of an established grant program rather than the launch of a new multibillion-euro initiative.

[Japan announces another Women in Fusion and Science training school](https://www.qst.go.jp/site/jt60/wifas2026.html). Japan's National Institutes for Quantum Science and Technology described a February 2027 program combining fusion and accelerator science lectures, facility visits, practical work, networking, and career support. Participation is planned for roughly 40 women students, researchers, and engineers, with no attendance fee and some travel support. This is a targeted workforce-development measure, rather than a reactor or financing milestone.

[Virginia officials outline the local preparations needed for CFS's planned ARC site](https://www.whro.org/environment/2026-10-15/is-nuclear-fusion-the-future-of-energy-in-virginia-some-officials-think-so). An October 15 regional report examines how Virginia's public agencies and local institutions are approaching the proposed ARC project in Chesterfield County. The discussion spans workforce, infrastructure, public engagement, and regulation in addition to economic-development hopes. No new construction start or power-plant result is reported. The value is in seeing the state and municipal work that a private fusion developer will need before it can proceed from site selection to an operating facility.

[ITER schedules another public-private fusion workshop for April 2027](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/4th-iter-public-private-workshop-announced). ITER says its fourth Public-Private Fusion Workshop will take place April 12–13, 2027, and aims to shift from broad exchange of information toward cooperation on specific technical problems. The question is whether companies gain practical access to experiments, engineering tools, models, and joint research projects. The announcement itself preceded the main news window on October 12, and no new collaboration contract has yet been reported.

[Zap Energy completes two more independently reviewed DOE milestones](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-10-13/article-8481/three-fusion-companies-in-does-milestone-program-have-updates/). The DOE Milestone-Based Fusion Development Program approved Zap's work on a high-repetition-rate pulsed-power capacitor and an internally manufactured prototype, according to the American Nuclear Society. The same October 13 report discusses Xcimer's additional agreement for up to $30.5 million, already covered in the October 14 Fortnightly. The Zap update is worth retaining because milestone verification ties part of federal fusion support to specific completed technical work rather than broad corporate announcements. The publicly available summary does not give a full independent engineering test report.

## People and hiring

[Fusion job openings rise to 817, led by CFS](https://www.fusionenergybase.com/articles/trends-in-fusion-hiring-october-2026). Fusion Energy Base counted 817 open positions across 58 fusion-related employers as of October 14, up 32% from about 620 in March. CFS led with 120, followed by Helion with 68, Xcimer with 54, and Proxima Fusion and Thea Energy with 52 each. Although physics remains the largest single job category at 131 openings, mechanical and electrical engineering together account for 225, and manufacturing and technicians for another 155. The tracker also shows that laser, optical, manufacturing, and electrical roles take longer to fill than many other categories. These are observed postings, not filled positions, but they suggest an increasingly hardware-intensive employment mix.

[Xcimer opens senior program-management positions as its defense work expands](https://xcimer.energy/careers/). Xcimer is recruiting a Head of PMO at a listed $241,000–$284,000 base salary and a Technical Project Manager for DOE Partnerships at $140,000–$165,000, along with senior systems-engineering roles. Its job descriptions explicitly say the company is moving from a single program toward a portfolio dominated by inertial fusion but including a growing defense business. That creates a need for common schedule, cost, risk, systems-integration, and security processes. These advertisements provide a more concrete view of organizational direction than an undated general statement about hiring.

[Oak Ridge seeks a Fusion Program Manager to coordinate its research and commercial work](https://doe.jobs/jobs/779778493-fusion-program-manager). An October 23 opening, requisition 16607, seeks someone to oversee program management, execution, sponsors, industry partnerships, and strategy across Oak Ridge National Laboratory's fusion portfolio. The job is significant because the laboratory's work spans materials, blankets, tritium, and other technologies required by multiple private developers. It is a leadership vacancy rather than the appointment of a new director, and the role's reporting structure and authority will matter as much as the title.

[Proxima Fusion recruits for site development and procurement](https://www.proximafusion.com/careers/open-position/site-development-program-manager-db5bc40c). Proxima advertised a Site Development Program Manager and Procurement Manager, with additional executive-office hiring among its October listings. The site role explicitly covers feasibility, permitting, utility and site-owner relationships, and the path toward investment decisions for facilities including Stellaris; procurement work covers supplier qualification and technically complex components. The hires suggest the German stellarator company is staffing the processes needed to build and supply an industrial project, not only its plasma and magnet design teams.

[General Fusion advertises a senior technical recruiting role](https://www.fusionenergybase.com/jobs/4185). General Fusion posted a Talent Acquisition Partner position in Richmond, British Columbia, with a listed salary of C$120,000–C$135,000. A dedicated technical recruiter is a modest signal of organizational hiring intentions as the company works on LM26 and its next-phase plans. A vacancy should not be interpreted as evidence of a large, financed hiring expansion; the company must still recruit and retain the employees.

[Gauss Fusion's Uwe Bau joins MIT's Plasma Science and Fusion Center as a visiting scientist](https://www.linkedin.com/company/gauss-fusion/). Gauss amplified an announcement that Uwe Bau received a FESTIM Fellowship and began a visiting-scientist appointment at MIT. The move reflects growing personnel exchange between commercial developers and academic plasma and tritium research groups. The announcement does not itself represent a new financing or corporate R&D partnership, but specialist placements can help carry engineering and materials know-how across organizations.

[Realta Fusion seeks a laser and photonics specialist for neutral-beam injection](https://realta-fusion-inc.prismhr-hire.com/job/1060038/nbi-scientist-laser-photonics). Realta's NBI Scientist, Laser & Photonics opening in Madison calls for an expert in high-power laser systems, resonant optical cavities, and photoneutralization for its HAMMIR neutral-beam-injection development. The role would design early cavity experiments, quantify optical losses and diagnostic needs, and connect the laser system to a future negative-ion beam. That is a relatively specific sign of subsystem development, though the job posting does not establish that a reactor-scale neutral-beam injector has been built.

[Type One Energy recruits an industrial control systems lead](https://typeoneenergy.com/careers/). An October 21 opening for a Senior Engineer, Industrial Control Systems Technical Lead in Knoxville points to the engineering disciplines needed for Project Infinity beyond stellarator optimization. Industrial controls tie electrical, mechanical, plant safety, and operating systems into a reliable facility. The posting indicates active recruitment for that capability but does not establish plant installation or commissioning progress.

[CFS recruits materials test operators and engineers in Devens](https://cfs.energy/careers). CFS advertised a Materials Test Operator contract position, at $35–$45 an hour, and a Summer 2027 Materials Test Engineer internship, at $25–$38 an hour. These roles sit in the less visible work of qualifying and testing reactor materials and components, which will require technicians and engineers as well as physicists. The openings are consistent with the wider increase in manufacturing and testing roles in the industry, though individual postings alone cannot establish the pace of SPARC or ARC progress.

[Marvel Fusion advertises scientific GPU infrastructure and AI venture roles](https://www.fusionenergybase.com/jobs). Marvel's October listings included a research software engineer for scientific computing and GPU infrastructure and an AI venture-development internship in Munich. Scientific-computing infrastructure can be as important to laser-fusion program productivity as individual simulation results, particularly when groups are running large optimization or design campaigns. The listings also show work on business development alongside the technical platform, without indicating a newly financed facility or device.

[Lawrence Livermore advertises new laser and fusion research positions](https://careers.llnl.gov/). LLNL's October 13 hiring activity included openings for laser-matter interaction experimentalists, laser and photonics scientists, experimental-physics postdocs, and extreme-light and fusion internships. The positions cover multiple career stages and technical disciplines across high-energy lasers and laser-plasma physics. Although the postings fall on the cutoff date of the preceding issue, they help show continued public-laboratory demand for personnel competing with private inertial-fusion developers.

[UCLA builds a Fusion Simulation Center and opens a project scientist position](https://recruit.apo.ucla.edu/JPF11413). UCLA's Department of Physics and Astronomy advertised an October 23 Project Scientist opening, with a salary range of $79,400–$209,000, to help establish a Fusion Simulation Center devoted to integrated steady-state tokamak and power-plant concepts. The job calls for simulation suites that combine plasma physics with plant engineering, including blankets, tritium breeding, materials, and energy extraction. It is a recruitment announcement, not a completed reactor design, but offers a more specific signal of new academic capability than a general plasma-physics vacancy.

[UKAEA's FOSTER program expands master's-level fusion teaching](https://www.ukaea.org/news/supporting-fusion-inclusion-into-postgraduate-education/). An October workforce update reports that four British universities are expanding master's-level fusion teaching with matched institutional support, scholarships, and new teaching posts under UKAEA's FOSTER program. This activity predates the current fortnight, but the staffing and course-development work is relevant to the industry's medium-term engineering talent supply. Teaching capacity and practical technical training will matter if fusion expands beyond small research and startup teams.

[UKAEA wins an engineering employer award](https://www.ukaea.org/news/ukaea-wins-large-employer-of-the-year-award/). UKAEA was named Large Employer of the Year at the 2026 Engineering Talent Awards, citing its efforts in engineering and technical workforce development. The recognition does not directly change the number of available fusion engineers, but it is one signal of how public programs are competing for talent alongside private companies. It is lower priority than actual hiring or newly funded training programs.

[Fusion Fuel Cycles highlights an early-career internship through Invest Ottawa](https://www.linkedin.com/company/fusion-fuel-cycles/). The Kyoto Fusioneering–Canadian Nuclear Laboratories fuel-cycle joint venture highlighted Queen's University co-op student Cece Zhou, who joined through Invest Ottawa's talent program. It is a small local example of expanding fusion talent pipelines beyond physics postdocs to undergraduate technical placements and regional employer partnerships. No new company appointment or research result was announced.

[Fusion Industry Association launches a People in Fusion interview series](https://www.fusionindustryassociation.org/). FIA introduced a monthly podcast focused on researchers, executives, policymakers, investors, and others working in fusion. It adds a potentially useful recurring source of personnel and institutional information but is not a senior hire or new fusion investment. The launch is worth noting principally as a way to follow a growing community that remains thinly covered by conventional energy journalism.

[Xcimer highlights Chief Scientist Cliff Thomas, appointed in September](https://xcimer.energy/news/xcimer-hires-university-of-rochester-fusion-scientist-cliff-thomas/). A recent social-media update revisited Xcimer's appointment of Cliff Thomas, previously of the University of Rochester's Laboratory for Laser Energetics, as chief scientist for its fusion program. Thomas brings target-design, simulation, and inertial-confinement expertise. The company originally announced the hire in September, so it should not be presented as a new October 22 appointment; it is included here only because the updated personnel research surfaced it during this reporting period.
