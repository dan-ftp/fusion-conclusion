## Companies, financing, and commercial development

[Helion and CFS to receive up to $260 million in CHIPS Act funding](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/10/department-commerce-announces-letters-intent-4-companies-389-million-rd). The U.S. Department of Commerce signed letters of intent to provide up to $175 million to Helion and $85 million to CFS, together accounting for two-thirds of a $389 million round of semiconductor R&D funding. Helion will continue to develop high-voltage equipment for its pulsed-power systems. The project also includes $50 million in private investment. CFS will develop power electronics, RF heating generators, fast plasma controllers, radiation-hardened electronics, and AI-related hardware for future ARC plants. CHIPS Act is funding these because it is within its mandate for the development of advanced semiconductors and domestic manufacturing capabilities.

A notable bit is that the federal government will receive minority, non-voting equity stakes in both companies in exchange for the funding. For Helion, the government would receive a $175 million equity interest valued at the company's most recent Series G financing; the terms for CFS have not been disclosed (I welcome my American taxpayer readers for joining me as new owners in CFS!). Specific technical milestones and payment conditions are not public. There's been some noise about this announcement around the administration in supporting Helion unfairly supporting family investments (by way of Kushner at Thrive Capital), which I find a bit overblown. As TAE is merging with Trump Media and Technology Group and has it's own power electronics business that is not being supported by CHIPS funding. In my opinion, the pulsed power work at Helion is one of the few things they are doing that is of genuine value. 

[Marathon Fusion reports on its first production of gold from mercury using fusion neutrons](https://x.com/MarathonFusion/status/2113001073019662481), improving a CERN benchmark by 10x. It also claims a new plasma-centrifuge isotope-enrichment milestone. 

[Hephaestus raises $17.2 million to make rare-earth-free HTS and negative triangularity tokamaks](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261020291741/en/). The company plans to start manufacturing the new HTS materials at a 70,000-square-foot facility in New Jersey in 2027 (starting I can see happening by then but not major production) and targets a Q>1 reactor demonstration by 2030 (essentially impossible). They claim to have discovered and fabricated novel rare-earth-free HTS materials using AI, with laboratory characterization by the UK's National Physical Laboratory. It has not disclosed any important information for assessing performance relevance for use in fusion devices. Rare-earth-free HTS materials already exist (e.g., Bi-2212) but matching REBCO's high-field performance and manufacturing scalability has not yet been met. The supply-chain argument is also somewhat overstated: 10,000 km of 4 mm REBCO tape (how much CFS recently announced purchasing that supplies ~1/2 and ARC) contains only around 80 kg of yttrium, making its raw-material cost insignificant (a few thousand dollars). Chinese export restrictions pose a procurement risk, but the much larger challenge for fusion is manufacturing sufficient quantities of affordable, high-quality HTS tape. Until we get quantitative performance data, it is impossible to assess whether Hephaestus has made a meaningful technical advance.

[Cambridge Heavy Industries raises $6.2 million for compact laser fusion](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/22/3386302/0/en/cambridge-heavy-industries-raises-6-2-million-seed-round-to-advance-its-modular-benchtop-scale-fusion-system-technology.html). The MIT-linked startup raised a seed round led by Azolla Ventures to build a compact experimental laser-fusion system in Massachusetts and expand its laser, optics, diagnostics, and engineering work. CHI says its approach can generate extreme target pressure with relatively small amounts of laser energy and has reported an approximately 350 GPa compression result using tens of millijoules. It is exploring tritium production, neutron imaging, and materials testing as intermediate markets. The relevant question is whether small, repeatable experiments translate into a useful neutron yield and ultimately economically relevant target gain; the prototype claims do not establish either.

[Gauss Fusion closes its Seed-to-A round and opens a Series A](https://gauss-fusion.com/news/gauss-fusion-closes-seed-to-a-financing-launches-series-a-fundraising). Gauss Fusion announced an undisclosed bridge financing and began raising a larger Series A for its GIGA stellarator program. Its engineering work centers on high-field magnets, tritium-breeding blankets, and power exhaust, with an intermediate demonstrator called GID-170. The company is presenting itself as a plant designer and integrator working with specialist suppliers rather than a manufacturer of every subsystem; its latest technical discussion covers demountable magnets, modular helium-cooled breeding blankets, and the PinkCap exhaust design. Those are meaningful reactor-architecture choices, but the figures Gauss cites for plant life and availability remain design targets rather than demonstrated results.

[Helion details the Omega capacitor manufacturing line](https://www.helionenergy.com/blog/inside-helions-capacitor-manufacturing-line). Helion says its new in-house Omega line integrates capacitor winding, assembly, vacuum drying, filling, testing, and module integration for the pulsed-power systems used by its fusion machines. It reports production capacity of roughly two capacitor modules per hour, about four times its earlier rate, and winding equipment rated above 100 joules of capacitor capacity per minute. That is significant because repeatedly charging and discharging large banks of capacitors at acceptable cost and reliability is a central requirement of Helion's architecture. These are company-reported manufacturing rates, not evidence of power-plant-level component lifetimes.

[Starlight Engine signs strategic investment agreement with a JR East-backed fund](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000014.000152790.html). Japan's Starlight Engine, linked to Kyoto Fusioneering and the FAST demonstration effort, announced an investment agreement with the TAKANAWA GATEWAY Global Co-Benefits Fund, managed by Global Brain and backed by JR East. The investment amount was not disclosed. Bringing a major rail and infrastructure company into the investor base could help Starlight with siting, construction, and industrial project execution, although it says little by itself about the technical maturity of its fusion concept or the financing still required for FAST.

[American Fusion reports four Texas data-center proposals and new financing discussions](https://americanfusionenergy.com/news/). American Fusion, which trades on the OTCQB market, reported four proposals involving Texas data centers, plans for a North Texas research campus, and discussions with investment banks. These are company-reported negotiations and proposals, not signed power-delivery agreements or evidence that its Texatron concept has produced usable electricity. The distinction matters given the company's previous promotional announcements about data-center demand. The next meaningful disclosures would be binding customer terms, a credible engineering demonstration, and financing sufficient to deliver the proposed facilities.

[Type One Energy revisits the idea of sharing tritium breeding across a fusion fleet](https://typeoneenergy.com/blog/inside-fusions-remarkable-fuel-cycle/). Type One's new article discusses its earlier peer-reviewed proposal for a future D-T fusion economy in which some plants breed more tritium than they consume and supply other plants that do not individually achieve self-sufficiency. Such a system might relax blanket constraints at certain sites, but it transfers some risk to fuel inventories, transport, processing losses, commissioning schedules, and dependence between plants. A fleet-level tritium economy is an interesting system-design argument rather than an experimentally demonstrated solution. The underlying paper is older than this reporting period.

[Helical Fusion agrees to a proposed investment through an individual-investor fund](https://www.fusionenergybase.com/news?topic=partnership). Japan's Helical Fusion signed a basic agreement with eCloud for an investment through a dedicated single-stock fund serving individual investors. The amount and definitive terms were not disclosed. The announcement follows its recently reported Series B closing, covered in the previous issue, and is interesting chiefly as a different channel for private fusion financing. A basic agreement should not be counted as a completed new financing round.

[Borealis Fusion launches with $6 million to pursue a proton-boron tandem mirror](https://borealisfusion.com/introducing-borealis-fusion/). Borealis announced a $6 million seed round on October 15 from investors including Astera, CRV, Breakthrough Energy Fellows, and several other funds and individuals. Founded by Damien Scott, Ian Ochs, Elijah Kolmes, Tal Rubin, and Alex Glasser, the company is developing PRISM, a proton-boron rotating-ion-separation mirror descended from tandem-mirror confinement. Several founders previously worked on the 1¢/kWh Fusion Energy study and Princeton-associated p–B11 research. The stated premise is that a linear, largely aneutronic plant could avoid costly D-T blanket and neutron-damage requirements, but the founders explicitly acknowledge that its required combination of confinement, radiation control, heating, and ash removal has not been demonstrated.

[First Light Fusion wins a UK defense contract for hypervelocity-impact research](https://firstlightfusion.com/media/first-light-fusion-selected-by-the-uk-defence-science-and-technology-laboratory-dstl-to-deliver-hypervelocity-impact-research-programme/). The UK Defence Science and Technology Laboratory selected First Light to fire steel projectiles into graphite at speeds exceeding 3 km/s and measure the response using its gas gun, pulsed power, diagnostics, simulations, and proprietary amplification technology. The program will reproduce and extend a previously published experiment; its contract value was not disclosed. The deal gives First Light a non-fusion customer for equipment developed through its inertial-fusion work and could support useful near-term revenue, while introducing the contractual and security obligations associated with defense research.

[Stellios Technologies raises $5 million for high-power microwave systems](https://thefusionreport.substack.com/p/this-weeks-fusion-news-october-23). Stellios has emerged with an oversubscribed $5 million pre-seed round and reports roughly $2 million in signed customer orders for megawatt-class microwave hardware. It is initially emphasizing counter-drone and other counter-electronics applications but identifies fusion plasma heating as a potential market. The attraction is a supplier able to develop and qualify high-power RF technology against paying customers outside fusion, rather than depending entirely on reactor developers' uncertain purchasing schedules.

[Chinese fusion supplier Hefei Keye reportedly raises a Series A](https://dealroom.co/news/165501-fusion-supplier-hefei-keye-raises-series-a-as-capital-bets-on-reactor-su/). A Dealroom report identifies a new venture round for Hefei Keye, a Chinese fusion-related supplier. The public account does not provide enough detail to establish the round size or precisely characterize the company's products. The financing is nonetheless an indicator that private capital in China is starting to reach the component supply chain as well as reactor builders, although a funding headline alone does not establish production capability.

[Two Indian startups pursue sharply different fusion concepts](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moodys%3Afb5d42e3226c6%3A0-founders-who-chose-the-nuclear-option/). A new profile describes Bengaluru-based Pranos Fusion, founded in 2024, developing a low-aspect-ratio tokamak program combining HTS magnets, control software, and its PRAGYA test device, alongside Anubal Fusion's proposed laser-driven proton-boron approach. Both remain early-stage efforts with limited public funding and experimental evidence. Their importance is in broadening the geographic and technical mix of private fusion development rather than establishing a new reactor-performance milestone.

## Research, plasma physics, and experiments

[SPARC researchers outline three independent ways to measure Q>1](https://www.aip.org/scilights/sparc-positioned-to-demonstrate-fusion-power-breakeven). A new diagnostics paper describes three approaches to measuring SPARC's fusion power: calibrated whole-machine neutron flux, neutron spectral and spatial diagnostics, and calculations using measured temperature, density, and fuel profiles. Independent methods will matter given how much attention a reported Qfus>1 result will attract and how readily one systematic error could otherwise affect its credibility. The AIP account says CFS has begun commissioning work and expects to operate SPARC in 2027. This paper describes a planned measurement strategy, not an achieved fusion-gain result.

[Wendelstein 7-X begins its OP2.4 experimental campaign](https://euro-fusion.org/eurofusion-news/wendelstein-7-x-starts-op2-4-experimental-campaign/). W7-X has started a roughly 12-week experimental campaign emphasizing long-pulse high-performance plasmas, fast ions, and exhaust. Stellarators' central commercial promise is steady-state operation without externally driven plasma current, so simultaneous progress on confinement, energetic particles, and heat removal is more valuable than isolated record-setting. The outcome will be measured by the discharges obtained and how well they validate transport and island-divertor models, rather than the campaign's start alone.

[EAST reports evidence of core-edge coupling driving energy transfer across turbulence scales](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30638). Researchers using collective Thomson scattering and Doppler backscattering report an edge-originating quasi-coherent mode that appears in the core and couples to smaller-scale fluctuations. Their interpretation points to forward transfer of fluctuation energy from ion to electron scales through resonant coupling, a potentially important departure from simple two-dimensional inverse-cascade intuitions. The behavior was observed in a high-confinement regime that the authors say avoids large ELMs and tolerates high-Z impurities. This is an experimental preprint, and the mechanism and its generality still need independent scrutiny.

[Researchers find exact nonsymmetric vacuum magnetic fields with nested toroidal surfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28466). Sengupta and coauthors give a constructive family of exact three-dimensional vacuum fields whose lines lie on nested toroidal flux surfaces without continuous spatial symmetry. Adjustable parameters set rotational transform and magnetic shear, including exactly constant irrational transform. The result supplies demanding analytical benchmarks for stellarator equilibrium and optimization codes. These are mathematical vacuum fields, not coil designs or finite-pressure reactor equilibria; no engineering or plasma-performance breakthrough follows automatically.

[An independent exact nonsymmetric vacuum field has nonzero rotational transform](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29921). Omar López constructs a closed-form, nowhere-zero vacuum field in a bounded solid torus, without continuous Euclidean symmetry but with nested flux surfaces and rotational transform of -1. Every off-axis line closes after one poloidal and one toroidal circuit. Posted one day after the Sengupta preprint, it provides a different exact test case for three-dimensional equilibrium codes and adds to the mathematical understanding of nonsymmetric magnetic fields. It remains far from establishing a practical stellarator with finite pressure, stable particles, heat exhaust, and buildable coils.

[New analysis places bounds on gains from non-Maxwellian fusion fuel distributions](https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/xhzy-hxdw). Onofrio, Ourabah, and Presilla analyze which particle-energy distributions maximize fusion reactivity at a fixed mean kinetic energy. Depending on the local curvature of the reaction-rate function, a single Maxwellian or a bimodal distribution can be optimal, producing useful bounds for D-D and D-T reactions. This is relevant to proposals that use tailored nonthermal tails to boost fusion rates. But maximizing the reaction rate at a specified average energy does not include the power needed to create and sustain those distributions, so it is not a calculation of net reactor gain.

[Liquid-metal blanket models find severe forces during rapid magnetic-field collapse](https://journals.aps.org/prfluids/abstract/10.1103/jd2g-7rg5). Zikanov, Ahmad, and Smolentsev simulate electrically conducting liquid metal in a channel exposed to a rapidly decaying magnetic field, as might occur in an off-normal tokamak event. The induced currents and Lorentz forces excite transient flows and standing Alfvén waves, with large calculated forces developing within milliseconds. The result highlights an often overlooked coupling between plasma disruptions and breeder-blanket mechanics. It is a simplified model rather than a complete reactor blanket transient, but it identifies conditions that a credible liquid-metal design and protection scheme must survive.

[Room-temperature targets explored as an alternative to cryogenic direct-drive capsules](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/aeb1db). Du and colleagues use computational optimization to examine laser direct-drive targets made from low-Z solids and hydrogen isotopes that can be handled at room temperature. A commercial inertial-fusion plant would need an economical way to manufacture and inject large numbers of targets without losing their required properties; cryogenic handling is one of the harder options. The modeling addresses that practical constraint, but a proposed target geometry is not evidence of ignition, commercially acceptable gain, or manufacturability at high repetition rate.

[ModeCast predicts tokamak MHD instability patterns using a learned surrogate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23844). Researchers trained ModeCast on more than 45,000 JOREK calculations to estimate linear MHD growth rates and the spatial form of modes with toroidal mode numbers one through four. They report inference more than 10,000 times faster than multicore JOREK runs, excluding the equilibrium calculation, and no unstable cases classified as stable in their held-out test set. Predicting the actual eigenmode structure is more useful than reporting a single scalar stability score. The open question is how reliably a model trained on simulations will generalize to measurements and to new regimes outside its training distribution.

[Modeling suggests W7-X-style island divertors may scale to much larger stellarators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23107). An EMC3-EIRENE study enlarges W7-X-like configurations by factors of two and four and evaluates how island-divertor exhaust behaves at higher power and size. At high separatrix density, the larger model devices develop strong self-baffling, with reported target densities exceeding 10^21 per cubic meter and peak heat flux below approximately 5 MW/m² in the modeled operating points. Heat and particle deposition separate more at larger size, but the authors do not find it necessarily fatal. The results are encouraging simulations, not experimental proof of power-plant-compatible exhaust.

[TMAP8 work extends tritium transport modeling toward integrated fuel cycles](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0920379626004758). Simon and collaborators report verification, validation, and benchmarking of the open-source TMAP8 modeling framework from material and component transport toward larger fuel-cycle calculations. A fusion plant needs quantitative predictions of tritium permeation, retention, processing losses, and inventory across many interconnected systems, yet these are often modeled separately. More consistent transport calculations can help close those gaps. Their usefulness still depends on validation against reactor-relevant materials, temperatures, irradiation, and realistic chemical environments.

[KSTAR researchers test AI equilibrium inference using ex-vessel magnetic diagnostics](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/aeb396). Using KSTAR measurements and reconstructions, researchers train a neural model to recover equilibrium parameters without relying on sensors inside the vessel, a potentially useful capability where DEMO-level neutron exposure makes internal diagnostics difficult. They report strong agreement with EFIT-derived targets and millisecond-scale inference. An important caveat is that part of the model's input is generated from the same EFIT framework used to set its labels, so the accuracy is an upper bound on performance with genuinely independent external sensors, not a demonstration that all required in-vessel diagnostics can already be eliminated.

[RFX-mod2 team validates model-predictive plasma equilibrium control in hardware-in-the-loop tests](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0920379626004825). De Tommasi and colleagues test a tokamak equilibrium controller on actual control hardware connected to a real-time representation of machine dynamics. This is the step between a controller that works in an offline simulation and one that can meet the timing, integration, and reliability requirements of a plasma experiment. The work addresses execution and verification rather than a new confinement result, and it is relevant to any future reactor that will need multiple coupled control loops operating without constant human intervention.

[A differentiable graph-based framework is applied to the PROCESS reactor systems code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22687). The authors analyze the dependency graph of coupled equations in PROCESS and use the JAX-based cottax framework to identify the smaller subsets that actually require iterative solution. The objective is faster, more reliable derivative-based multidisciplinary optimization instead of relying on large numbers of parameter sweeps. This is a methodological advance in systems modeling, and its value will depend on whether the transformed code retains credible physics, constraints, and numerical behavior across the actual design search.

[Review finds no demonstrated practical quantum advantage for fusion-plasma simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25885). Zhu and colleagues survey quantum computing approaches to kinetic models, waves, equilibria, and stability in magnetic-confinement fusion. They find that the literature still concentrates on reduced or linearized problems and selected numerical subroutines, with state preparation, measurement overhead, and hardware limitations obstructing practical advantage on full plasma simulations. The paper is a useful correction to predictions that quantum computers are about to replace classical fusion codes. It also identifies research avenues that might eventually benefit, while making clear that such benefits remain unproved.

[A DIII-D study reconstructs unobserved core density using a neural-operator transport model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24525). Sorokin and collaborators combine a particle-transport equation with a Fourier Neural Operator surrogate and uncertainty quantification to infer core electron density from incomplete measurements. That is relevant to reactors in which diagnostic access will be scarce but controllers still need credible core profiles. The method retains an explicit transport model while accelerating repeated forward calculations with a learned surrogate. Tests include synthetic and DIII-D cases, but performance on unfamiliar machines and heating or fueling conditions remains to be shown.

[Tokamak-SI releases a labeled catalog of DIII-D discharge phenomena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32264). The Tokamak-SI project brings time-aligned labels for confinement states, detachment, Alfvén eigenmodes, ELMs, sawteeth, tearing modes, and resistive-wall modes into a common DIII-D dataset. Machine-learning groups have often worked from narrow, privately curated event labels that make comparisons difficult. Shared catalogs could support more realistic discharge interpretation and reproducible benchmarking, although differences in instruments, regimes, and labeling conventions make cross-device generalization an open problem.

[Writhe becomes an explicit variable in stellarator geometry optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18190). Schuett and Henneberg propose constraining the writhe of three-dimensional curves during stellarator optimization. This gives designers a measure of geometric complexity that may otherwise drift upward as they pursue desirable magnetic properties. Lower-complexity boundaries and coils could ease manufacturing and maintenance, but the new mathematical control is not itself a demonstrated reduction in magnet cost or increase in reactor performance. The preprint was posted October 13, just before this issue's main reporting window.

[Six-fluid equilibria model rotating, nonthermal proton-boron spherical-torus plasmas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16304). Peng and Ishida develop a force-balance description with separate thermal and suprathermal proton, boron, and electron components, including relativistic electron effects and rotation. Those distinctions matter for p–B11 concepts that assume energetic, non-Maxwellian particle populations not captured by ordinary single-fluid models. Mathematical equilibria are a useful consistency check, but this work does not show how to create, stabilize, or efficiently heat such a plasma. The preprint dates to October 12 and is included as a recent technical item, not a new experimental milestone.

[Coherent Ising machines are tested on fusion current-profile inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23507). Researchers combine a coherent-Ising-machine formulation with block coordinate descent and demonstrate a parameter-inference workflow connected to EAST current-profile reconstruction. They report millisecond-scale computations for the test formulation. The important limitation is that existing hardware does not have enough effective capacity to solve the complete problem in one pass, making the result more a test of a promising specialized optimization approach than a proven new real-time fusion-control system.

[Atomic modeling quantifies how irradiation defects reduce tungsten thermal conductivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26502). Chessey and colleagues use atomistic simulations and machine learning to link vacancy concentrations in tungsten to loss of lattice thermal conductivity. Their calculated response is nonlinear and suggests that phonon-scattering degradation can begin to saturate beyond a particular defect concentration. Reduced heat conduction raises temperatures and stresses in plasma-facing structures, so a better relationship between defects and thermal properties is useful. These are atomistic predictions, however, rather than neutron-exposed component lifetime data.

[KSTAR develops a temperature-response method to check whether cooling channels have dried](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0920379626004795). Engineers use transient temperature behavior to assess residual water and drying in plasma-facing-component cooling channels. The problem is unglamorous but relevant to conditioning, vacuum preparation, and maintenance on heavily water-cooled machines. The paper illustrates how fusion engineering increasingly needs reliable procedures for inspecting, conditioning, and returning components to service rather than only new plasma operating regimes. It appeared October 13.

[Kazakhstan's KTM tokamak reports its first divertor discharges](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0920379626004874). A new Fusion Engineering and Design paper describes initial diverted operation of KTM, including machine operating features and first experimental observations. Operating with a divertor enables new studies of plasma boundary geometry, heat exhaust, and plasma-facing components. It is an early commissioning result, not a high-performance confinement record, and the paper was published October 13. Nonetheless, KTM adds useful experimental variety to a field dominated in English-language coverage by a small set of larger facilities.

## Supply chain, manufacturing, and enabling technology

[ITER completes its first series-production divertor inner vertical target](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/first-production-unit-completed-europe). Germany's Research Instruments has completed the first production inner target after more than a decade of development and qualification. Each target carries 2,208 machined tungsten blocks joined to water-cooled tubes and must handle heat loads as high as 20 MW/m². More than 60 companies contributed to the first unit; Research Instruments has orders for 28, and Europe is responsible for 58 including spares. The immediate test is repeatable fabrication, quality control, and delivery of the remaining assemblies, not whether one prototype could be made.

[Fusion for Energy signs first IFMIF-DONES procurement arrangements for an injector and EUROFER steel](https://fusionforenergy.europa.eu/news/europe-signs-its-first-procurement-arrangements-for-ifmif-dones/). The first two European in-kind supply agreements for the fusion-neutron materials-testing facility cover its deuteron-beam injector and approximately 15 tonnes of reduced-activation EUROFER97 steel. The injector draws on work at the Europe-Japan LIPAc accelerator, while EUROFER will support structures and systems that must ultimately face high neutron fluence. F4E says the arrangements begin to turn its roughly 25% project contribution into concrete work packages. Materials irradiation data from DONES will be important for credible lifetime predictions and qualification of future fusion blankets and structures.

[Brookhaven and Great Sky test a superconducting neural-network chip on fusion-plasma data](https://www.bnl.gov/newsroom/news.php?a=123222). Brookhaven, Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory, DIII-D, and semiconductor startup Great Sky have demonstrated superconducting neural-network circuitry on fusion-relevant anomaly and disruption-prediction tasks. The test processed DIII-D data sampled at one million readings per second, according to Brookhaven. The main result is a specialized hardware demonstration, not a new plasma controller installed on a reactor. Faster processing close to sensors could be useful as machines demand low-latency protection and control, but deployment and radiation tolerance remain separate engineering questions.

[ITER releases the JOREK nonlinear plasma-instability code as open source](https://euro-fusion.org/eurofusion-news/jorek-a-plasma-simulation-code-is-now-open-source/). JOREK is an established tool for modeling ELMs, disruptions, tearing modes, shattered-pellet mitigation, runaway electrons, and associated loads on tokamak components. Opening its source lowers the barrier to auditing algorithms, benchmarking simulations, and expanding the code's physics. It could also make machine-learning surrogates trained on JOREK easier to reproduce. Open availability should not be confused with a fully validated reactor-scale disruption-prediction capability; that will still require experimental comparison.

[DOE designates helium a critical material](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-designates-helium-critical-material-strengthen-american-manufacturing). The Energy Department's October 23 designation recognizes helium as strategically important to U.S. manufacturing and subject to supply risk. Helium is used for cryogenic superconducting systems, semiconductor manufacturing, and vacuum leak testing, all relevant to fusion development. Disruptions to overseas production and trade can therefore affect fusion projects even when magnets and other specialized components are available. The designation is a policy step, not an immediate new helium-production capacity or a guarantee against shortages.

[ITER's European procurement authority seeks €23.8 million of quality-control inspection services](https://industryportal.f4e.europa.eu/f4e-calls/). Fusion for Energy's October 14 tender, F4E-OMF-2050, calls for quality-control inspectors for ITER, Broader Approach, and roadmap projects, with a December 2 deadline and an estimated value of €23.8 million. These services cover the inspection burden that comes with complex components, multinational subcontracting, and exacting fabrication requirements. The contract has not been awarded. Alongside ITER's first production divertor inner target, this is a useful measure of how much fusion construction depends on ordinary industrial disciplines such as metrology, traceability, and supplier inspection.

[Swiss supplier Ampegon delivers further electron-cyclotron power supplies for ITER](https://fusionforenergy.europa.eu/news/swiss-expertise-to-power-up-iter/). Fusion for Energy reports additional handover of high-power equipment from Ampegon for ITER's electron-cyclotron heating system and an agreement for two more units. The supplies form part of the RF infrastructure needed for plasma heating and control, where generators, power conversion, protection, and transmission equipment must work together reliably. This is a real procurement and delivery milestone from an industrial supplier, rather than an experimental heating-performance result.

[NSTX-U receives a 23,000-pound central magnet bundle from Spanish manufacturer Elytt](https://www.ans.org/news/topic-fusion/step-1782306296/). The Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory upgrade received a large central magnet assembly built by Elytt Energy in Bilbao, Spain, combining toroidal-field and ohmic-heating functions. The delivery is a reminder that major fusion hardware comes through an international supply chain with difficult fabrication, transportation, installation, and acceptance-testing steps. Physical delivery does not mean the magnet has been installed, commissioned, or used in plasma operations.

[Fermilab describes its MAGNES superconducting cable and magnet test capability](https://news.fnal.gov/2026/10/physics-and-fusion-research-advances-through-fermilabs-magnes/). MAGNES draws on Fermilab's accelerator-magnet experience to test conductors, cable designs, and magnetic systems relevant to high-field applications including fusion. Qualified independent test capacity is valuable as private reactor developers and HTS manufacturers move from small samples to much larger assemblies. The October 12 update falls just before the main reporting window but is relevant to the supply chain, particularly because winding a magnet is only one part of demonstrating the required electrical, mechanical, and cryogenic margins.

[Next Step Fusion joins Proxima Fusion's Alpha Alliance as a diagnostics supplier](https://fusion-future.com/news/next-step-fusion-brings-plasma-diagnostics-to-proximas-alpha-alliance). Next Step Fusion has joined the supplier alliance forming around Proxima's Alpha stellarator, adding plasma diagnostic capabilities to the group's magnet, manufacturing, cryogenic, and vacuum expertise. The agreement is significant as an example of reactor builders assembling specialized suppliers early rather than developing every subsystem in-house. Its importance will depend on actual work packages and the qualification of diagnostics under Alpha's expected plasma and machine conditions.

[IS-Instruments demonstrates measurement of all six hydrogen isotopologues with one Raman system](https://is-instruments.com/hydrogen-isotope-analysis-notus-grade-3/). UK diagnostics supplier IS-Instruments reports that its Notus instrument, developed through a UKAEA-supported program, can simultaneously analyze H2, HD, HT, D2, DT, and T2. That capability could be useful for online composition measurements in fusion fuel processing, recycling, and accountancy. The result addresses a practical need distinct from plasma diagnostics: knowing which hydrogen isotopes are where in a chemically and radiologically complicated plant. Industrial deployment still requires qualification for real process streams and operating conditions.

[DOE reports that lithium application methods change hydrogen-isotope retention](https://www.energy.gov/science/fes/articles/limiting-fuel-retention-lithium-future-fusion-devices). Research highlighted by DOE finds that precoated lithium surfaces retained less deuterium than surfaces treated by lithium-powder injection in the studied conditions, while changes in layer thickness had a smaller effect. This matters for any plasma-facing strategy using lithium because retained tritium affects fuel inventory, safety limits, and maintenance. It also points to a materials-process problem: the way a coating is applied may matter as much as the nominal material selected.

[EUROFER97 qualification work advances structural design rules for fusion blankets](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnucmat.2026.157169). A new Journal of Nuclear Materials study reviews EUROFER97 qualification and validation of design rules for ITER test blanket modules, including evidence needed to extend allowable operating conditions in RCC-MRx. It addresses the difficult step between having a candidate reduced-activation steel and being able to specify it in engineered components with accepted stress and fabrication criteria. Qualification of materials and design codes is likely to remain a constraint for breeding blankets even after enough raw material can be produced.

[Laser-based inspection can itself change deuterium retention in tungsten coatings](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/aeb81b). A Nuclear Fusion study of laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy finds that diagnostic laser pulses can heat or modify deuterium-loaded tungsten surfaces and change the amount of deuterium that remains. The experiments combine LIBS and nuclear-reaction analysis on coatings tested in Magnum-PSI. LIBS could be attractive for remote fuel-retention surveys, but a measurement that perturbs the sample needs calibrated interpretation. This is a materials-metrology problem with direct relevance to tritium accountancy during maintenance.

[Alumina coatings cut hydrogen-isotope permeation through stainless steel](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fusengdes.2026.116099). A newly indexed Fusion Engineering and Design paper examines one-micrometer Al2O3 coatings deposited on 316L stainless steel by pulsed-laser deposition to reduce hydrogen-isotope permeation. Tests with deuterium at 400–500 °C found permeation-reduction factors of roughly 860–2,500 relative to uncoated samples. This is useful progress for future tritium-handling piping and blanket systems, where leakage and inventory must be controlled. The larger qualification problems remain coating defects, adhesion, fatigue, repair, and behavior under irradiation and repeated thermal cycles. The authors circulated an earlier preprint in February.

[ITER researchers publish a control framework for ion-cyclotron heating](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0920379626004849). A newly posted Fusion Engineering and Design paper describes simplified models and initial control functions for ITER's ion-cyclotron heating equipment. The researchers built a dedicated heating-system model, plasma-coupling emulator, and event-handling procedures within ITER's plasma-control simulation platform. The problem couples high-power RF supplies and antennas to diagnostics and plasma control under tight operating limits. It is a modest engineering result but shows the work required to deliver plasma heating as an integrated, qualified plant subsystem rather than a stand-alone RF experiment.

[Chinese Startorus test uses domestically supplied REBCO in a fast-ramping central-solenoid model](https://www.eastsuper.com/newsshow.php?id=909). A supplier report dated October 10 describes testing a model central solenoid at 20 K, reaching a reported peak field of 10 T and ramp rate of 63 T/s. Eastern Superconducting Technology says it supplied all of the REBCO tape. The interesting engineering point is field ramp rate and conductor performance in a dynamic magnet, not just static field. This is a manufacturer-reported test released before the strict news window, with limited independent supporting data.

[Kronos Fusion publishes a design-only down-selection of compact tokamak divertor concepts](https://www.kronosfusionenergy.com/publications/a-divertor-exhaust-concept-down-select-for-a-compact-sp.html). Kronos has published a comparative study of Super-X, snowflake, X-point-target, and liquid-metal exhaust geometries for its proposed compact spherical-tokamak breeder, as highlighted in an October 14 LinkedIn post. The company favors strong flux expansion and near-total edge radiation, with an assumed radiated fraction of about 0.998. That stringent detachment requirement is beyond demonstrated operating conditions and remains a named technical gap in Kronos's own materials. This is an open design-and-simulation study, not a built or experimentally qualified divertor, so its status is fundamentally different from ITER's production hardware milestone.

[Fusion for Energy publishes a report on building Europe's fusion supply chain](https://fusionforenergy.europa.eu/news/how-did-f4e-build-the-european-fusion-supply-chain/). F4E has released an industrial analysis of the specialist suppliers, production capabilities, and quality systems developed through almost two decades of European ITER contracts. The report treats procurement as a means of building industrial capacity, not simply purchasing parts. It is relevant to the increasing overlap between public facilities and commercial fusion firms, although the historical spending and capabilities described should not be confused with new funding committed this fortnight.

[Japan faces a potential yttrium supply squeeze as Chinese export restrictions tighten](https://www.ft.com/content/ebd3244a-9120-4773-b55f-41df77f60c93). The Financial Times reports concern in Japan that yttrium availability could become constrained within months. The immediate industries affected include semiconductors, aerospace, and high-temperature coatings, not a reported shortage of fusion HTS tape. But many fusion supply chains depend on rare-earth and semiconductor processing with concentrated international sources. The story is an upstream procurement warning and should not be taken as evidence that any named fusion project has suffered a material shortfall.

## Government, regulation, and public programs

[UK Fusion Energy's annual report reveals STEP spending, supplier growth, and governance weaknesses](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-fusion-energy-ltd-annual-report-and-accounts-202526/uk-fusion-energy-ltd-annual-report-and-accounts-202526-accessible-version). UK Fusion Energy reports £118.8 million of 2025–26 program spending, 371 British suppliers, and roughly 80% of expenditure retained domestically, alongside 70 technology demonstrations and 29 patent applications. The annual report also identifies noncompliance with delegated authority under a Fusion Partner Agreement, gaps in work-package oversight, fragmented intellectual-property management, and unclear asset ownership. STEP is progressing environmental review and site-control arrangements at West Burton. The figures show that public procurement is building a substantial supply chain, while the audit findings suggest that contract controls, IP ownership, and program governance are already meaningful risks to the state-backed project.

[Israel proposes three levels of investment in a national fusion strategy](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/nuclear-fusion-strategy). Israel's Ministry of Energy and Infrastructure published options ranging from NIS 50–100 million over five years for research and training to a NIS 1–2 billion national program over seven to ten years that could involve a larger experimental facility. A middle option proposes research infrastructure and public-private funding on a smaller scale. The ministry identifies lasers, RF, power electronics, materials, AI, and robotics as areas where Israeli industry could contribute to the global fusion supply chain. These are options open for public comment, reportedly until December 10, not appropriated funding or contracts awarded to domestic fusion companies.

[Shanghai's new industrial plan explicitly backs fusion equipment](https://sheitc.sh.gov.cn/cyfz/20261026/246991fa89514b9db6dcb9afb47a6e7c.html). Shanghai's 2027–29 energy-equipment action plan includes high-temperature-superconducting tokamaks and other experimental fusion-device technologies among its targeted sectors. The plan goes beyond plasma research to cover first-of-a-kind applications, industrial development, standards, financing, and manufacturing. This is a municipal economic-development plan, not a disclosure of a new plasma result or a single national funding round. It is useful evidence that China's fusion programs are building local industrial ecosystems in parallel with their reactor research.

[Virginia radiation advisers warn they have no dedicated resources for fusion oversight](https://townhall.virginia.gov/l/GetFile.cfm?File=Meeting%5C58%5C44056%5CMinutes_VDH_44056_v1.pdf). Draft minutes of the October 7 Radiation Advisory Board meeting report that Virginia's Office of Radiological Health is already spending staff time on fusion-related regulatory issues without dedicated funding. Members questioned whether fees collected from existing licensees should subsidize fusion work and discussed uncertainties around anticipated activities by Commonwealth Fusion Systems. The meeting also described broader staffing and budget strain. The minutes were posted during this reporting period and are an unusual glimpse of a practical licensing bottleneck: state agencies can face real workload long before the first plant is built.

[Max Planck and Columbia establish a $13.3 million fusion research center](https://www.mpg.de/27042432/max-planck-columbia-center). The five-year Max Planck Columbia Center, beginning in 2027, will connect Columbia University with Wendelstein 7-X, ASDEX Upgrade, and German and U.S. plasma research groups. Its program covers stellarator exhaust, tokamak-stellarator hybrids, transients, non-planar superconducting magnets, energetic particles, and edge stability. CFS, Proxima Fusion, Thea Energy, and Type One Energy are among companies expected to be invited into working groups. Carlos Paz-Soldan and Thomas Klinger will co-direct the initiative, which also plans exchanges of graduate students and postdocs. The funding is modest compared with private reactor programs, but shared experimental facilities and verified modeling could have broader value.

[IFMIF-DONES partners approve a 2027 program and revised international governance terms](https://ifmif-dones.es/dones-updates/dones-steering-committee-drives-the-programme-into-a-new-phase-with-the-approval-of-key-frameworks-for-implementati/). The multinational DONES Steering Committee approved its 2027 work program, updates to the multilateral agreement and committee rules, and provisions for admitting additional observers. It also reported that construction activity on the main facility and plant systems in Granada had begun. These steps matter because DONES depends on coordinated national contributions and eventual operation as a neutron-irradiation test facility for fusion materials. They are governance and program-execution milestones, not new neutron-exposure data.

[Legal experts warn that public consultation may be a critical path for fusion permitting](https://www.fusionindustryassociation.org/felex-publishes-discussion-paper-on-public-engagement-and-social-acceptance-of-fusion/). FELEX, a group of specialists in fusion law and regulation, published a comparative discussion of public participation and acceptance. It argues for engagement before formal licensing, clearer information about differing national consultation requirements, and possible community benefits or participation mechanisms. No new law was enacted. The point is that a relatively light radiation-safety regime does not eliminate land-use, environmental, local consent, or procedural obstacles, particularly for proposed first-of-a-kind power stations.

[Japan's science minister visits Kyoto Fusioneering's fuel-cycle and gyrotron work](https://kyotofusioneering.com/en/news/2026/10/26/4936). Science and technology minister Toshiharu Furukawa toured Kyoto Fusioneering's development activities on October 22 and discussed the company's expansion into industrial fuel-cycle, plasma-heating, and related technologies. The company's growing network of test facilities and international sales makes it an example of a Japanese fusion supplier building businesses before electricity-producing reactors exist. The visit is a political and industrial-policy signal, not new financial support or a technical performance result.

[EUROfusion names recipients of its 2026 researcher and engineering grants](https://euro-fusion.org/eurofusion-news/2026-researcher-and-engineering-grants/). EUROfusion announced awards to early-career scientists and engineers working at member institutions, supporting research and technical training related to Europe's fusion roadmap. Such programs have a much smaller budget than reactor construction, but they address a real supply constraint in specialized engineering and experimental skills. The announcement identifies recipients of an established grant program rather than the launch of a new multibillion-euro initiative.

[Japan announces another Women in Fusion and Science training school](https://www.qst.go.jp/site/jt60/wifas2026.html). Japan's National Institutes for Quantum Science and Technology described a February 2027 program combining fusion and accelerator science lectures, facility visits, practical work, networking, and career support. Participation is planned for roughly 40 women students, researchers, and engineers, with no attendance fee and some travel support. This is a targeted workforce-development measure, rather than a reactor or financing milestone.

[Virginia officials outline the local preparations needed for CFS's planned ARC site](https://www.whro.org/environment/2026-10-15/is-nuclear-fusion-the-future-of-energy-in-virginia-some-officials-think-so). An October 15 regional report examines how Virginia's public agencies and local institutions are approaching the proposed ARC project in Chesterfield County. The discussion spans workforce, infrastructure, public engagement, and regulation in addition to economic-development hopes. No new construction start or power-plant result is reported. The value is in seeing the state and municipal work that a private fusion developer will need before it can proceed from site selection to an operating facility.

[ITER schedules another public-private fusion workshop for April 2027](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/4th-iter-public-private-workshop-announced). ITER says its fourth Public-Private Fusion Workshop will take place April 12–13, 2027, and aims to shift from broad exchange of information toward cooperation on specific technical problems. The question is whether companies gain practical access to experiments, engineering tools, models, and joint research projects. The announcement itself preceded the main news window on October 12, and no new collaboration contract has yet been reported.

[Zap Energy completes two more independently reviewed DOE milestones](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-10-13/article-8481/three-fusion-companies-in-does-milestone-program-have-updates/). The DOE Milestone-Based Fusion Development Program approved Zap's work on a high-repetition-rate pulsed-power capacitor and an internally manufactured prototype, according to the American Nuclear Society. ## People and hiring

[Fusion job openings rise to 817, led by CFS](https://www.fusionenergybase.com/articles/trends-in-fusion-hiring-october-2026). CFS led with 120, followed by Helion with 68, Xcimer with 54, and Proxima Fusion and Thea Energy with 52 each. Although physics remains the largest single job category at 131 openings, mechanical and electrical engineering together account for 225, and manufacturing and technicians for another 155. The tracker also shows that laser, optical, manufacturing, and electrical roles take longer to fill than many other categories. These are observed postings, not filled positions, but they suggest an increasingly hardware-intensive employment mix.
